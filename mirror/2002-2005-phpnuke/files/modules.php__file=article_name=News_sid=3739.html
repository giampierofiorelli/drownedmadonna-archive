Guy's producer to become director

LONDON At 33, Matthew Vaughn has already made two movies that helped define a new genre, the hip British gangster flick. He has made millions of dollars for himself and his main financial backer, Sony Pictures. He is married to one of the world's most glamorous women, the model Claudia Schiffer.

You might expect him to swagger a bit - if not like a Hollywood studio boss, then perhaps like one of the drug lords who populate his films. Yet before a screening of Vaughn's latest picture, "Layer Cake," which is set for release in Britain this autumn, he is modest, almost nervous.

"Remember, it's just a small English film," he says.

Measured by its budget of £4 million, or $7.3 million, "Layer Cake" is indeed small. But then so were "Lock, Stock and Two Smoking Barrels" and "Snatch," two films that Vaughn produced that went on to be international box office hits.

The reason for Vaughn's trepidation is that "Layer Cake" signifies his debut as a director and is the first film from his new London-based firm, MARV. While Vaughn produced "Lock, Stock" and "Snatch," both were directed by Guy Ritchie, Madonna's husband, under the two men's joint venture, Ska Films.

With the better-known Ritchie now pursuing his own projects in America, Vaughn is eager to make his own mark as a director while keeping his reputation for understanding profits and losses as well as sets and actors

"Lock, Stock" was shot on a £960,000 budget but took in roughly £35 million in revenue in Britain from the box office, home video, television and other sources. Globally, it has earned around $80 million.

"Snatch" had a bigger budget, about £5 million, in part because the cast included Brad Pitt, but it turned into an even bigger worldwide moneymaker. It has taken in more than $80 million at the box office and a comparable amount in video sales and other revenue.

Along with bigger-budget films like "Bridget Jones's Diary" and "Four Weddings and a Funeral," from Working Title, a London-based production house backed by Universal Studios, the Vaughn-Ritchie movies have helped fueled a revival of British cinema in recent years.

Originally, Ritchie was to direct "Layer Cake." But when he decided to focus on his own projects, Vaughn decided to direct it, even though he is colorblind and had had no experience as a director

Because the most recent collaboration between Vaughn and Ritchie, the critically panned "Swept Away," was a flop, there has been speculation of a rift between the two. Vaughn, however, plays down such talk.

"We still get on, thank God," Vaughn said. "I can't produce anything with him now - maybe in a few years."

Source: International Herald Tribune.
