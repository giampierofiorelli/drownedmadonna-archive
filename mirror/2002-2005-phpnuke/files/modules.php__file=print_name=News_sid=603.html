Chicago Tribune: EMI may acquire Warner Music.
Date: Tuesday, September 23 @ W. Europe Daylight Time
Topic: Magazines

EMI Group PLC, home to old-time rockers the Rolling Stones and jazz star Norah Jones, said on Monday it had opened formal talks to buy AOL Time Warner Inc.'s music business in a deal that could be worth more than $1.5 billion.

An EMI-Warner Music combination would create a music giant second only to Universal Music, with a roster spanning The Beatles, Frank Sinatra, Madonna and Christina Aguilera, as the industry scrambles to find a quick fix to sliding music sales.

"Discussions are at a very preliminary stage, and there is no assurance that they will result in an agreement acceptable to both parties," EMI said, confirming Reuters reports that EMI was courting Time Warner as rival talks with Bertelsmann faltered.

EMI and Warner Music, the world's third- and fourth-biggest music companies, tried to merge three years ago but failed after stumbling at the regulatory gate.

With sales sliding, the music companies hope regulators will be more lenient this time around, but independent labels said they will lobby against a deal.

Sources familiar with the situation told Reuters last week that EMI had refreshed a bid it made for Warner Music earlier this year, which included $1 billion of cash and would give AOL Time Warner a 25 percent stake in a combination.

London-based EMI's shares ended 4.1 percent lower, adding to an initial decline amid uncertainty over the outcome of the Warner talks, after Standard & Poor's placed the company's ratings on CreditWatch with negative implications.

"This can only be encouraging for EMI investors, as it offers the prospect of cost savings. But we'll have to wait and see how much they end up paying," said Investec analyst Kingsley Wilson.

AOL Time Warner, soon to return to its original name Time Warner, decided last week to open the field of contenders for Warner Music after struggling to wrap up a deal with German rival Bertelsmann, people familiar with the situation said.

However, talks between AOL Time Warner and Bertelsmann over a 50-50 recorded music joint venture are continuing.

"The talks have lost momentum, but the negotiations are far from dead," said one source familiar with the talks.

AOL Time Warner and Bertelsmann declined to comment.

Facing a gloomy outlook, neither Warner Music, EMI nor Bertelsmann's music arm BMG wants to be left out in the next round of consolidation as each attempts to slash costs.

AOL Time Warner, which has already sold its CD and DVD manufacturing business, also has been under pressure to pare its long-term debt, which stood at $26 billion as of June 30.

However, getting a music merger through regulators remains a big hurdle, and independent labels said they would oppose a deal.
