New interesting ''Swept Away'' review.
Date: Tuesday, June 03 @ W. Europe Daylight Time
Topic: Magazines

Italian “Carnet” of May publishes an article by Claudio Barabba about Madonna and "Swept Away" ("Travolti dal Destino"). A very worth reading, because it presents few interesting points with progressive critics and questions. Read more...


Take your hands off Madonna. Probably it is true that her new movie “Swept Away”, free remake of an ancient and fairly erotic comedy by Lina Wertmuller (Travolti…, 1964), is an absurd spoof story. In America it has been alighted from cinemas in a day or two, and it has received raspberries (the golden ones, the negative awards handed out to Benigni for his “Pinocchio”). Maybe you remember the plot: a middle - class, bossy and spoilt rich Lady, (in the original one played by Mariangela Melato, high - toned Lady), is shipwrecked in a too much light sea together with a farmyard sailor (Adriano Giannini took his plentiful father’s place, the more famous Giancarlo). After the class struggle discussions, it will born a brief and rough corporal love. To tell the truth, the original, even if lucky, wasn’t a masterpiece and Giannini, the father was good, but too much excessive. The younger Adriano, grappling with the bulky family ghost, is likely to get lost and talented Guy Ritchie, adventure director and Madonna’s husband, did not find his pace. From this point to preventive lynching, there is a big difference. According to many critics, in fact, Madonna has always been one of the worst actresses in Hollywood and environs. I absolutely disagree with it. The girl seemed self - confident to me in the comedy of her debut (Desperately Seeking Susan, 1985), later she was a sorry and charming victim in the dark “Snake Eyes” by Abel Ferrara, a suited handspring acrobat in “Shadows and fog” by Woody Allen, and a warm and passionate spotlight chaser in the vibrant “Evita” by Alan Parker. In conclusion, a carrier worthy of respect. In comparison with her, especially in America, very loved stars, like Jennifer Lopez and Sandra Bullock, seem two housemaids. And so, in the climate of witch hunt, that incited the singer to withdraw her new video (American Life, in which she joked about the mania of war) rises the suspect of a puritan aversion, a wish of ostracism which in the Bush II era is in danger of extend itself to several rebel artists. At the head of the suspected there is an ex partner of Madonna herself, the restless Sean Penn, a ductile and extrovert actor, and an original and brave author. The last fault of Penn is the fact he directed a lyric and unusual short about the collapse of Twin Towers (the final act of a collective movie dedicated to September 11th) and the fact he took sides against the bombs over Bagdad. Other famous names would appear in a black list already drafted by some USA magazine: if they continue to disturb, they could run the risk of losing their job. Fifty years ago they called it McCarthyism.
