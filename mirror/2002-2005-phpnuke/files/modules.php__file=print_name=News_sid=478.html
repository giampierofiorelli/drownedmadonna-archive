Why Madonna's favourite yogic exercises are in danger of bringing Britain...
Date: Tuesday, August 12 @ W. Europe Daylight Time
Topic: Magazines

... to its knees.

They are the A-list exercise fads followed by everyone from Claudia Schiffer to Madonna. But now, it seems, yoga and Pilates could be bad for you.

The Consumers' Association (CA) is warning that a lack of regulation means untrained and inexperienced teachers are leaving people injured from badly run classes. Osteopaths and chiropractors say their consulting rooms are flooded with people who have suffered neck, knee and back damage as a result of yoga and Pilates.

The CA is calling for much tighter regulation of the disciplines, with proper training structures before people can set up as instructors.

The soaring popularity of "body and soul" exercise regimes such as Pilates and yoga has led to demand for classes outstripping supply.

The British Wheel of Yoga (BWY), which covers some forms of the discipline, has 2,100 teachers who teach an estimated 500,000 pupils.

Pilates has also become a boom business. Its popularity is so great that even Ms Schiffer had to join a 13-week waiting list when she signed up for classes. The England cricket team travels with its own Pilates instructor. But the discipline has no regulatory body and no recognised training structure, meaning anyone can set up him or herself as a teacher, even with no experience.

Pilates includes the use of machines and stretching exercises, which can cause muscle strain and tissue damage if performed wrongly.

The BWY regulates some forms of its discipline, such as Hatha yoga, but two of the most popular forms, Ashtanga and Bikram, do not come under the remit of the BWY and are completely unregulated.

Jeanette Thomson, an osteopath who works in north London, said: "I am seeing tons of people who have injured themselves doing yoga or Pilates. I am just stunned that people are setting themselves up as teachers when they have no experience or qualifications, and that there is nothing to stop them doing so.

"I spoke to someone last week who said he had been made redundant from the City and had decided to set himself up as a yoga instructor - but he had never even gone to a class."

Ms Thomson added: "People are coming in with lower back problems, neck injuries and trouble with their knees. When I start questioning them, it is obvious that it has happened at a yoga or Pilates class ...I see a lot of people who say their instructors were pushing them on the machines, or encouraging them to stretch a bit further.

"The combination of dodgy teachers and a general public who are not warming up properly or taking care of themselves is simply disastrous."

Monica Burton, chairwoman of the BWY, said: "Proper yoga teachers do 500 hours of training before they instruct other people, yet some gyms send their aerobics instructors on a day-long course and then get them to run yoga classes."

Alan Herdman, who brought Pilates to Britain from America in the early 1970s, is now pushing for tighter regulation of the discipline.

THE CELEBRITY CHOICE

Enlightenment and serenity are no doubt in high demand in the world of actress Trudi Styler.

But between mothering her four children, saving the world's rainforests and matchmaking her friend Madonna, Sting's wife still finds time for several hours of yoga a day. Her husband is just as devoted to Ashtanga yoga and the couple have a designated yoga room in their mansion.

But even with the best teachers and 10 years' experience, the regime can be punishing. Two years ago Ms Styler snapped her sternum after a particularly strenuous bout of Ashtanga.
