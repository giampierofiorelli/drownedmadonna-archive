The Guardian: A moral message from Madonna - at £12.99 a copy.
Date: Monday, September 15 @ W. Europe Daylight Time
Topic: Magazines

Madonna, the pop star who inspired millions of young girls to answer back to their parents, today completes her latest career transformation as 1 million copies of her new morality tale for children are published worldwide.

"The English Roses" has already found its way into publishing history as the widest, simultaneous multi-language release, with a target of more than 100 countries in 30 languages. The US print run alone is 400,000.

The massive hype of Madonna's simple fable perplexed the publishing world yesterday as she hosted an elaborate Kensington tea-party launch without a single copy of the book. Around 100 children turned up, including a class from North Ealing primary school and a batch of celebrity offspring. They were instructed to make sculptures, hats and bags based on the book and learnt a dance routine from Lynn Page, who choreographed the film Billy Elliot. Parents such as the TV chefs, Nigella Lawson and Gary Rhodes and the author Martin Amis, were met with more than 1,000 rose garlands, floating clouds, plenty of champagne and stilt-walkers dressed as swans. But no hard copies of the product.

Instead, Madonna, 45, climbed on to a wrought iron garden-swing, with her children Lourdes, 6, and Rocco, 2, and read for five minutes, announcing that anyone who wanted to learn the ending must shell out £12.99.

"The English Roses" is the first of five morality tales by Madonna to be released before the end of 2004. All are based loosely on the teachings of the Kabbalah, which she follows. The fashion artist, Jeffrey Fulvimari, has illustrated the first book and other artists will follow.

"The English Roses" is the tale of a stereotypically beautiful blonde girl called Binah, who is picked on by a pack of four other girls who feel jealous of her, until they find out she has no mother and a miserably sad existence.

But the book's tie-ins might put off the more moralistic. The global clothing chain, the Gap, which has been forced to deny criticisms of using Asian sweatshops to produce its clothes and for whom Madonna has done an advertising campaign, has made special English Roses bags for the launch and will stock the book.

Outside the tea party, Madonna said: "I'm not interested in being recognised as a writer, I'm interested in getting the message out there. The book deals with jealousy, envy and being covet of what other people have and what a waste of time that is."
