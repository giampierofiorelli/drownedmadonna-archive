SWEPT AWAY - an interview with Guy Ritchie *PART 2*

Here's the second part of the long heart-felt interview given by Guy Ritchie to Michael Fleming of Movieline magazine.

Enjoy the conversation and check back soon to find the third final part of the interview.

 

 



 

MF: Describe one of those pitfalls.

GR: However you slice, it’s Madonna. It’s hard for people to digest that they are not looking at Madonna. Once you deal with someone who is rather controlling from the offset, who has a larger-than-life personality, at least you feel comfortable from that position. Than you can slowly transform that personality.

MF: This is not a catwalk, in terms of what you put your wife through. Does such an experience place a strain in your marriage?

GR: Funnily enough, it didn’t. We got on very well during the whole process. It was just work, she acted very professionally. I was very happy. Outside of that, it just made it all rather convenient because you could talk about things over dinner.

MF: Madonna is known for being a perfectionist. Who asked for more takes, you or her?

GR: We struck a happy balance. She trusts that I’m not going to make her look like a twit. I trust her in that I know she’s going to memorize her lines.

MF: How do you explain her film career?

GR: Some of the films that she’s done, they were essentially meant to be small pictures. Studios ride on  the fact that she is Madonna and that they’re going to get a lot of publicity because of who she is, and turn what is essentially a small picture into a commercial picture. Which it wasn’t meant to be. Then it ends up being rather embarrassing for everyone.

MF: Why do you think people have been so critical of her acting?

GR: She is a very capable woman. She is a very capable actress. She pays attention and she has heart and soul. What goes against that is the fact that she is Madonna. That works against her, in terms of people digesting her work. Truth is, if she wasn’t Madonna, no one would ever say, “isn’t she a terrible actress?”. You can’t really blame people, it’s just the way we’re designed.

MF: The British press was hard on her performance for the London play Up For Grabs.

GR: It was so fucking…I was there on opening night. Tell you what, however you slice it…I’m not biased, genuinely not. I know when she’s doing’ something that is not very good and I know when she is doing something that is good. She was just magnificent-there was no question- on opening night. She was magnificent and there’s no one that was going to say anything different apart from these begrudging critics that were going to kick the shit out of her. I don’t know what the fack she would have had to do for them to give her a round of applause. As someone trying to be objective, this was a very, very good performance. If it was anyone else, they would have said it was a very, very good performance. They haven’t been cruel, but it’s fucking begrudging. It is something that has to be taken into consideration. But she doesn’t read the papers and so she doesn’t know and so she doesn’t care. She doesn’t want to do a bad job, she cares about doing a competent, very good job. She wants to do everything she does very well and she does do that well.

MF: You’ve mostly directed men. Is there a different set of muscles you have to use to direct women?

GR: Not really. Truth is, I am a guy’s guy and I like doing guy things. I’m more drawn to that. But I’m heterosexual. I love ladies and I’m interested in that kind of stuff, too.
MF: Martin Scorsese is married and female characters were never his strength. I’m not casting aspersions on your sexuality or anything…

GR: I understand what you mean, it’s a sensible question. I didn’t find it challenging in that respect. Ladies are ladies, I deal with them in the respect of how I understand them.

MF: Was part of the reason you cast Adriano Giannini as Madonna’s lover because he’s the son of Giancarlo Giannini, who starred in the original “Swept Away”?

GR: I looked at a whole floor of Italian actors and I picked Adriano because he looked right. I had no idea who his father was and it wasn’t till we were on the plane ride over to the shot when someone pointed it out. The only thing about that was it seemed a bit cute. But he was by far the best we saw. He’s very capable, he’s a man-the quintessential Italian lover. He’s got a good body, he’s funny, he’s  a great actor.

MF: It was reported that John Turturro was going to play the part. What happened?
