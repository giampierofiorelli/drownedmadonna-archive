Charts
Britain's most played songs of the past 70 years.
THEY are Britain's most played songs of the past 70 years.

The list, topped by Procol Harum's 1967 hit A Whiter Shade Of Pale, includes songs by such obvious candidates as the Beatles, Queen and Robbie Williams.

But the big surprise is the number of successful artists who do not make it onto the chart.

There is no place for Frank Sinatra, despite countless plays of My Way, while Tom Jones loses out too, even though his '60s hits It's Not Unusual and Green Green Grass Of Home are still popular.

The Bee Gees' string of No.1 hits fails to win them a place, and '90s favourites such as Take That and the Spice Girls are also missing.

Some of the songs that do appear are more likely to have listeners tuning out, including Wet Wet Wet's Love Is All Around and Bryan Adams' 1991 hit (Everything I Do) I Do It For You.

The list of most-played records has been compiled by Phonographic Performance Ltd (PPL), which keeps tabs on all public performances and collects royalties on behalf of the artists.

It gathered data not only from radio, but from shops and supermarkets, jukeboxes, lifts, pubs and clubs.

The most popular artist was Robbie Williams, with five entries.

The Beatles have three and one-time rivals the Rolling Stones just one, with Honky Tonk Women at 42.

John Lennon has a solo entry with Imagine, but Sir Paul McCartney was not so lucky.

Elvis Presley has two songs in the list, All Shook Up and It's Now Or Never, the same number as Madonna.

The list of 70 songs has been compiled to mark the 70th anniversary of PPL.

The organisation said that it had devised the chart to ensure that all decades received a fair representation, although songs from more recent times are in the majority.

Source: The Sunday Telegraph.
