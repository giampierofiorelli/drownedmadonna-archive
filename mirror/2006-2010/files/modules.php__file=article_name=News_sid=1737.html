Evita's Burial Shroud on Auction.

ROME (AFP) - A flimsy burial shroud used to wrap the body of Argentina's political icon Eva Peron is expected to fetch more than 40,000 euros (50,000 dollars) when it goes on sale in Rome on Thursday, Christie's auction house said.


AFP/File Photo

 

The macabre garment will be among a collection of memorabilia from her husband, late Argentine president Juan Peron, to be auctioned in the Italian capital, the proceeds of which will go to a Latin American charity.

The Foundation for Peace and Friendship between Peoples, a charity which owns the memorabilia, hopes to raise more than 350,000 euros from the 60-lot sale.

"We decided to auction the shroud because Evita in her will authorised the use of all her belongings on condition that it could be used to help poor people," its president Antonio Rotundo told AFP Tuesday.

Other Evita memorabilia in the sale comprise a gold-framed dressing table mirror, a leather travel folding photo-frame, a hand-mirror, a music box in working order, and several unmatched and tarnished spoons. Christie's have posted a reserve price of 8,000 euros for the lot.

But her husband's memorabilia dominate. Furniture, official and family photos, a collection of records, uniforms, clothes, correspondence, personal diaries and more than a thousands books from Peron's personal library will be among the items on sale.

His blue gold-braided dress uniform is expected to fetch around 6,000 euros, as is the overcoat in which he made his last public appearance before his death in June 1974. Five pairs of his shoes are expected to fetch 500 euros.

But it is the diaphanous blue and white veils -- used to wrap the embalmed body of his extremely popular wife after it was exhumed from a secret grave in Milan, Italy two decades after her death in Buenos Aires -- which are expected to be of most interest to buyers.

Eva, revered by Argentines as "Evita" whose life story was transformed into an Andrew Lloyd Webber musical in the 1980s and a film featuring Madonna, shot to prominence when she married the then-minister for war in 1944.

An actress, and the object of almost mystical adoration, she helped found the Peronist Women's Party, as well as giving her name to a charity for the nation's poor.

Her death from cancer at the age of 33 in July 1952 traumatised ordinary Argentines. Close to a million crowded the streets of Buenos Aires for her funeral procession, and an estimated three million filed past her coffin to pay their last respects.

Overthrown in a coup in 1955, Peron went into exile in Madrid. Eva's body, which he had preserved in the hope of putting it on permanent display in a mausoleum, was stolen by the military junta which overthrew him.

It was later secretly taken out of the country and buried at a cemetery in Milan, Italy, under a false name. There it remained before it was taken back to the family tomb in Buenos Aires in the mid-1970s. The burial garment being auctioned dates from that time.

With Argentina in a political shambles, Peron had returned to power in 1973.

He died the following year, and was succeeded by his third wife Isabel, a nightclub singer he had married 12 years previously. Some of her memorabilia are also on sale in Rome.

Isabel called for Eva's body to be repatriated, and she was eventually laid to rest in a cemetery in Buenos Aires.
