Madonna deserves the chance to shine as an adoptive mom

Madonna & child: Leave them alone

Every now and then, I think it might be the bee's knees to be adored by millions. Of course, I'd have to "hide" from the paparazzi, batting my eyes from behind huge sunglasses that recall the bygone era of Breakfast at Tiffany's.

But there is another side of celebrity, a side that does not shine and is not fabulous.

Under the headline "Shameless Star Buys an African Souvenir," New York Post columnist Andrea Peyser had this to say of one celebrity's pending adoption of a 13-month-old boy in Malawi:

"Madonna, the sluttish, egomaniacal mother-of-the-century has topped even her most revolting self. She plans to remove a baby from the loving arms of his dirt-poor father, in one of the most desperate nations on earth.

"The one-named wonder, who already has given birth to two children by two different daddies, one of whom she would not deign to marry, has her heart set on raping Malawi."

That, my friends, is the journalistic equivalent of a drive-by shooting.

Madonna the celebrity is inextricably woven into Madonna the woman, and no matter what she tries to do, she seems a hopeless victim of her own mega-marketing.

Attacking Madonna for baring her midriff and hanging herself on a cross is one thing, that's showbiz. But to attack her for wanting to mother a motherless child, that's cruel.

Since the announcement a couple weeks ago that Madonna was following in the footsteps of other A-listers, most notably Angelina Jolie, and adopting a child from Africa, media watchers began taking potshots.

Would Peyser and her pious peers be quite so venomous had baby David come from Russia with love? Or better yet, if he were a Shanghai surprise?

When news spread that an international watchdog group was protesting the adoption and planning to challenge the legality, the child's biological father, Yohane Banda, had this to say:

"Where were these people when David was struggling in the orphanage? These so-called human rights groups should leave my baby alone. As father I have OK'd this, I have no problem. The village has no problem. Who are they to cause trouble? Please let them stop."

However, after who knows how many human rights activists had a chance to bend Banda's ear, now he is saying something else. Banda told The Associated Press: "If we were told that she wants to take the baby as her own we could not have consented, because I see no reason why I should give away my son."

Banda, who farms onions and tomatoes, lost his wife and two other children to malaria. His son, David, was in an orphanage where Banda could visit, but since he wasn't able to care for the child, he was not raising the boy.

So, what's a Madonna to do? If the superstar, mega-trendsetter truly has love in her heart and wants to expand her family, God bless her.

My only criticism of international adoption seekers is I wish they'd give domestic adoptions a chance. Consider adopting children whose love won't cost thousands in airfare and tens of thousands in fees.

In Florida, 2,906 children await adoption (136 in Broward County, 172 in Palm Beach County). In New York state, where Post pundit Peyser poured out her outrage over Madonna's adoption, 4,387 children await adoptive homes.

The bottom line:

With so many American kids needing homes, anyone with a lot of love and patience can be a rock-star mom. One child's love can feel like a stadium full of screaming fans. And if you're lucky, you may want to go back for an encore.

I did. And it made me feel like a celebrity. And an unsung hero. All at the same time.

No matter how many tours Madonna has sold out, she deserves the chance to shine as an adoptive mom.

Rock on, Madonna. Rock on.

Source: Sherri Winston, Florida Sun Sentinel
