BURGHLEY WINDOW DISPLAY: Madonna display is just horse play.
A LIGHT-hearted dig at music mega-star Madonna has won a coveted prize for a charity shop.
Visitors to Stamford High Street, in the past week have been tickled by the humorous window display of the Oxfam shop, showing a mock-up of a horse-rider sprawled on the floor.

Above the colourful display is a banner, which reads "Madonna fails to qualify for the Burghley Horse Trials" – in reference to the Queen of Pop's well-publicised fall from a horse last month.

The light-hearted display has drawn numerous compliments from shoppers and the shop has now won Burghley's Shop Window Display of the Year prize, which comes with two four-day passes to the high-society event, which finishes on Sunday.

Sue Thompson, assistant manager at the shop, explained how the idea arose.

She said: "We were thinking of ideas when one of our staff, who is a big Madonna fan, mentioned she had fallen off her horse.
"It seemed quite timely, given the horse trials were coming to the town.
"It's very much in a jokey spirit from fellow horse-riders and we wish her well with her recovery."

Madonna's management company were not available for comment.

[Read It First Here] Madonna For New Charity Auction .

Celebrities including Madonna and Kylie Minogue have backed a campaign to build an orphanage in memory of murdered schoolgirl Rosie May Storrie

Rosie May's parents, Graham and Mary, from Bottesford, aim to raise £30,000 to help victims of the Boxing Day tsunami after witnessing the devastation at first hand.

They are holding a charity auction to raise, money with items donated by a number of stars.

More than 30 items are up for auction after Mary and Graham sent letters to dozens of celebrities asking for help.

Madonna donated a signed copy of her CD American Life and one of "The English Roses " book.

Rosie May, who was 10 when she was murdered at a Christmas party in 2003, was a talented ballet dancer.

AmFar Wants Madonna In Activists Photo Album.

NYPOST - Liz Smith

KENNETH COLE, the handsome, smart new chairman of the board of AmFAR, was out in Hollywood last Oscar weekend. We ran into him at Elton John's jammed-to-the-rafters fete. But Kenneth, accompanied by his terrific wife, Maria Cuomo, wasn't there simply to party. He was networking and corralling stars for his big idea — a photograph, which will be published in December on World AIDS Day, of all the prominent scientists and activists who have given time, money, heart and soul to the cause. Mark Seliger is taking the pictures. So far Kenneth — who is putting his own money into this effort — has snared Greg Louganis, Tom Hanks, Rosie O'Donnell, Elton John and David Furnish, Desmond Tutu, Dr. David Ho, Dr. Suniti Solomon, Harry Belfonte, Sharon Stone and, but of course, Miss Elizabeth Taylor, the woman who started it all back in 1985 when she co-founded AmFAR with Dr. Mathilde Krim. Dr. Krim's in the photo, too, natch. Madonna is in Cole's sight-line, among others. (Stars who can't get together at the same time will later be digitally joined in the massive picture.)
