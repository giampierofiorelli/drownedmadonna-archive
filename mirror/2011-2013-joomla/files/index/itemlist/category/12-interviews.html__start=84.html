Berlusconi's Party attacks Madonna with rubbish and homophobia

Written by Giampiero Fiorelli
Tweet
Share on Tumblr


We told you that Madonna remarks about Italian Prime Minister Silvio Berlusconi were going to cause a lot of controversies. And so it was. Asked about her opinion on Berlusconi, Madonna replied "I wouldn't like to talk about it now. But English weekly magazine The Economist already said everything, isn't it?" And then Madonna laconically added that Berlusconi "is unsuited to govern Italy."

 

Now Berlusconi's political alignment toke sides against Madonna, including some Ministers. Instead of thinking about the economic crisis, they waste their time to talk rubbish about Madonna. It's incredible!

 

"Lady Ciccone is openly all for gay families," Undersecretary Carlo Giovanardi says. "Therefore she is taking sides against our culture and Constitution, which do not provide for gay families."

Madonna "is a person of no importance. Italians know that she is an immensely billionaire, so... people do not let these rich, spoilt and petty tyrants fool them," Giovanardi adds. Due to what happen in the UK in mid August, Giovanardi says that English people, and so an English newspaper, "cannot teach us anything."

 

Ex showgirl and now PDL politician Gabriella Carlucci says that Madonna cannot judge a country she doesn't know, she "cannot judge by hearsay, because somebody put words into your mouth during the gala dinner of your movie."

"I think it's a very wrong behaviour," Carlucci adds. "Hypocritical... it's absolutely a very, very, very wrong behaviour." Carlucci also said that artists like Madonna have a communist press office. Therefore there's no doubt they go to these dinners where they are unilaterally indoctrinated. "It's a pitty Madonna took such a tumble, especially in a country where she wants to promote her movie. I wouldn't take the liberty of criticizing a country where I am a guest."

 

Massimo Corsaro, Vice-President of Berlusconi's Party at the Chamber of Deputies, also wants to reply to Madonna, and says that she used this controversy to promote her movie W.E.

Corsaro threatens revenge: "We'll take away some of her records from the stores."

"To be honest, we don't care of Lady Madonna at all," Corsaro adds. "The treatment that Hollywood reserved for George Bush is indicative, and so, when she comes in Italy, Madonna feels justified in talking about Berlusconi."

 

Also Daniela Santanchè, Undersecretary of Cabinet Office, wants to reply to Madonna. "I'm very sorry that Madonna uses these words about Italy, because she is Italian... her surname is Ciccone," says Santanchè. "I hope that, as well as she has a poor opinion of Italy and Italian Government, Italians have a poor opinion of Madonna and her movie... and so she will be the only one to go to the theatres and watch it."

Santanchè adds that Madonna attacked Italy and Berlusconi to promote her movie. Stars like Madonna "use this and arouse controversy to publicize themself, their movies or their songs." Santanchè thinks that this is unethical.

Madonna praises Sean Penn and Guy Ritchie for their talent.

Written by Giampiero Fiorelli
Tweet
Share on Tumblr


Talking with Daily Mail, Madonna admits she learnt a lot, as a director, from being around and watching her exes Sean Penn and Guy Ritchie. "I mean, they're both talented film-makers and intelligent human beings," she says. "I was paying attention to what they were doing and watched. I'm a watcher and a learner."

 

Even though Sean Penn reportedly didn't spend good words for Madonna lately, she has lovely comments about him. At the end, Sean is still probably her biggest love. "I was amazed how seriously he took his parts and how far he was willing to go," she says. This taught her the importance of research when directing a movie.

 

Madonna has great words for Guy Ritchie as well. "I think Guy's a very stylish director and he does things with camera that are unconventional and certainly pushes the envelope in that area," she says. "In terms of visualising things, he's a ground-breaker."

Madonna shares what she has finally learnt: Compromise!

Written by Giampiero Fiorelli
Tweet
Share on Tumblr


In what Daily Mail's Baz Bamigboye describes as a rare intimate interview with a diva these days, Madonna was in a reflective mood, and confesses that everything she has now - a family, a career and a lover - as come at a price: compromise. She says that you can have all three, in fact she says that she has and she is "lucky,"  but "you can't then expect is a good night's sleep."

 

Madonna has finally learnt to art of compromising. "I can’t contemplate living without all three. I can’t imagine not having children. I can’t imagine not being in love and I can’t imagine not being creative and doing the work I do," she confesses. Therefore "Either the kids aren’t going to be getting all your attention, or the man isn’t or the job isn’t."

 

"You can’t always get what you want, but compromise pays off in the end," she adds. Even "Madonna has to beg sometimes. Madonna doesn’t get everything she wants."

Madonna denies saying Lady Gaga is obsessed with her to Belgian magazine Le Soir

Tweet

There is a formal denial to what Madonna reportedly said about Lady Gaga during an interview she gave to Belgian magazine Le Soir, while Madonna was in Venice for the Film Festival.

 

Madonna's spokesperson Liz Rosenberg denies the quote about Lady Gaga. "This is not true. Madonna did not say anything negative about Lady Gaga during her press conference," she told the Daily Mail. But, unfortunately this false news already went global.

Madonna: 'Being a mother is harder than being a director'

Written by Giampiero Fiorelli
Tweet
Share on Tumblr


Interviewed by Italian magazine Gente, out next Monday, Madonna confesses that being a director is a strenuous work, "but not as much as being a mother."

"Children tie you down for your whole life," says Madonna. "While a movie, sooner or later, ends."

Madonna also talks about her second movie W.E., "I started thinking about this storyline when I married Guy Ritchie in 2000 and I moved in England. As Wallis, I also felt alone and I felt like an outsider. Then she has been treated basically as a bitch. Just like all the women that over the centuries dared to defy man rules."

Love is also part of the interview, with Madonna saying that "women keep on thinking that happiness depends on finding and marrying the Prince Charming. I do not believe it. I think happiness is in our own hands, and only when we understand it we can find completeness in a partner."
