Madonna's W.E. "most definitely has the fashion world clamoring and bracing itself for a revival of classic and elegant heritage luxury brands such as Vionnet and Mainbocher," Huffinghton Post Style columnist Malcolm Harris writes.

Finally, Harris adds that "thanks to the Material Girl there is one thing we know for sure -- the world is about to get a lot more elegant, sophisticated and yes -- very, very romantic."

Harvey Nichols showcasing Vionnet pieces worn in Madonna's W.E.

Written by Giampiero Fiorelli
Tweet
Share on Tumblr


London's Harvey Nichols is showcasing a selection of Vionnet pieces worn in Madonna's second director fatigue, W.E., as seen on Andrea Riseborough.

 

"The film W.E. gave Vionnet such a wonderful opportunity to showcase the exquisite craftsmanship and skill of the house," the label's ceo Matteo Marzotto told Vogue. "We were thrilled to be considered for the project and it was so exciting to be part of such a lavish production that portrayed one of history's most intriguing women. The film, W.E., challenged us to utilise our extensive knowledge and craftsmanship to re-create the original pieces designed by Madeleine Vionnet for Wallis Simpson with precision and accuracy. Extensive research was carried out on fabrics and construction using original pieces from the archives at the Louvre."

 

The chosen pieces will be shown on London's Harvey Nichols' first floor, alongside the current autumn/winter 2011-12 collection.

 

"Harvey Nichols is such a visionary retailer with a world renowned reputation so it is such a pleasure that Vionnet can work with them on this installation," said Marzotto. "The store is the perfect place to showcase the intricate and exquisite pieces and will allow customers to truly appreciate the attention to detail and craftsmanship of the house, we hope that the installation provides a greater awareness of the unique heritage and vision of Madeleine Vionnet."

 

The installation will be on display until Friday September 30.

Mohamed Al-Fayed, Sean Penn and Guy Ritchie for Madonna's W.E.

Written by Giampiero Fiorelli
Tweet
Share on Tumblr


In an interesting interview by the Philippine Daily Enquirer, Madonna talks about her talking with her exes Guy Ritchie and Sean Penn about her film W.E., and negotiating with Mohamed Al-Fayed.

 

Madonna had to get the permission of Mohamed Al-Fayed to film at the former home of King Edward III and Wallis Simpson in the Bois de Boulogne, and read some of the letters between the Duke and the Duchess Al-Fayed is in possession.

"I wanted to not only film there but also to use his likeness in the film. So I needed to get his permission," Madonna says. "I also knew he was in possession of many of the letters between the Duke and the Duchess. So I had many reasons to want to meet him.

"He was extremely generous and forthcoming. As soon as I came into his office, he opened up books and pages of letters to let me look at. He didn’t let me walk away with any of them. We had several meetings.

"He wanted to see the script. He wanted to know how he was portrayed so I had to give him the scenes that were written about him. And they met his approval (laughing).

"Then he gave me permission. I agreed to help him with the charity that his daughter was in charge of, a school for underprivileged and abused children in England. So it was a tradeoff. I helped him with his daughter’s charity and he let me film in his house. I think that was fair trade."

 

Madonna also told the Enquirer of her talks with her former husbands Sean Penn and Guy Ritchie about her movie W.E.

"I never showed the script to Sean. I only talked to him about making the film," reveals Madonna. "He’s always been supportive of me as a creative person."

As for Guy Ritchie, she tells that when she was writing the script, and probably they still were married, "I did give the screenplay to Guy at a certain point. I also shared the concepts, stories and ideas with Guy because he was also interested in the story from a historical point of view."

"They never really gave me specific advice about telling this story but Guy would give me technical advice about cameras or using digital versus film or one cinematographer being better than another or one sound mixer being better than another," Madonna adds.

 

Madonna also talked about the filmmakers who inspired her especially on W.E.

" I was inspired by Ingmar Bergman’s film “Persona” because it’s the story of two women who have this symbiotic relationship and, in many ways, they switch positions. By the end of the movie, you’re not sure who’s the crazy one and who isn’t," Madonna confesses. "The nature of their relationship was interesting to me."

"I’m also a really big fan of Alain Resnais and his film, “Last Year at Marienbad.” I loved his use of camera movement and filming in long tracking shots down hallways and mirrors. That film was cutting edge for its time. It broke a lot of rules about filmmaking," Madonna says. "All the actors would show up on the set every day. None of them even had a script but they were so in awe of the director that they were willing to just be there, wait and hear what he wanted them to do. I was fascinated by the fact that the actors were willing to take this risk and that he was willing to take that risk, too. He was a big inspiration."

Madonna could re-edit some scenes of W.E. ready by the BFI London Film Festival to make the film be a contender at the Oscars

Written by Giampiero Fiorelli
Tweet
Share on Tumblr


In a previous news we brough to your attention that, while she was in Italy for the Venice Film Festival 2011, in an interview with Daily Mail's Baz Bamigboye, Madonna revealed that W.E. maybe "is still a work in progresse," and that, after the screening gala at the TIFF Toronto Film Festival on September 12, she could decide to change or add some scene. "I would like to think that the film is finished, but I did watch it the other night [in Venice] and I thought: 'Um, maybe it needs a bit of this and a bit of that.'" For instance there is a "sexy" steamy scene or two between Wally and a Russian security guard that was been cut, as were others, for movie lenght reasons, and now maybe Madonna will change her mind.

"Now it has been shown at the Venice and Toronto festivals, Madonna has a brief window of opportunity to decide how to shape her movie to best advantage," reports Baz. "If Madonna wants W.E. to be a contender this awards season, she must act fast and have a new version ready by the UK premiere at the BFI London Film Festival on October 23." "We see shots of a lot of corridors, so they can go, and other scenes can be trimmed,’ the executive told Baz.
