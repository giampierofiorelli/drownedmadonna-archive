Beth Ditto performs Madonna's Vogue at NYC Fashion's Night Out

Tweet
Share on Tumblr


Last night was a special night in New York City and Milan for all fashion victims: it was the Fashion Night Out.

 

Gossip's frontwoman Beth Ditto performed at M.A.C. Cosmetics store in Soho, NYC, to promote her cool EP, which is a homage to the 90's. "First of all, house music, especially from the Nineties, is the gayest music ever," she told Rolling Stone. "It's all drum and bass, which is perfect for parties."

 

During the gig she also she performed Madonna's Vogue, "complete with a troupe of gesticulating, eye-liner-laden males writhing behind her," reports the Rolling Stone. "The response? Uproarious."

 

 

Last night gig was to promote Beth Ditto's EP, whose first single, I Wrote the Book, video absolutely has a Vogue-ish vibe as well some reference to Justify My Love... and we really like it!

 

 

It was not the first time that Beth Ditto enjoyed performing Vogue, for instance, she also did it at London's Lovebox Festival and in Moscow's Miller party.

 

So Beth Ditto loves Madonna. But is the feeling mutual? Reportedly Madonna loves Beth, and in July Madonna's manager Guy Oseary twetted: "I just showed Madonna the video of Beth Ditto performing Vogue at Moscow Miller party, She thought Beth was great..."

Madonna praises Sean Penn and Guy Ritchie for their talent.

Tweet
Share on Tumblr


Talking with Daily Mail, Madonna admits she learnt a lot, as a director, from being around and watching her exes Sean Penn and Guy Ritchie. "I mean, they're both talented film-makers and intelligent human beings," she says. "I was paying attention to what they were doing and watched. I'm a watcher and a learner."

 

Even though Sean Penn reportedly didn't spend good words for Madonna lately, she has lovely comments about him. At the end, Sean is still probably her biggest love. "I was amazed how seriously he took his parts and how far he was willing to go," she says. This taught her the importance of research when directing a movie.

 

Madonna has great words for Guy Ritchie as well. "I think Guy's a very stylish director and he does things with camera that are unconventional and certainly pushes the envelope in that area," she says. "In terms of visualising things, he's a ground-breaker."

Madonna shares what she has finally learnt: Compromise!

Tweet
Share on Tumblr


In what Daily Mail's Baz Bamigboye describes as a rare intimate interview with a diva these days, Madonna was in a reflective mood, and confesses that everything she has now - a family, a career and a lover - as come at a price: compromise. She says that you can have all three, in fact she says that she has and she is "lucky,"  but "you can't then expect is a good night's sleep."

 

Madonna has finally learnt to art of compromising. "I can’t contemplate living without all three. I can’t imagine not having children. "You can’t always get what you want, but compromise pays off in the end," she adds. Even "Madonna has to beg sometimes. Madonna doesn’t get everything she wants."

The Screen: The trashing British Press response to W.E. is pre-ordained

Tweet
Share on Tumblr


The Screen International published a very interesting article on their online version about Madonna's W.E. and the international press response, especially the British press.

Mike Goodridge's observations are absolutely to the point and shareable.

 

Goodridge starts from the assumption that being Madonna is not easy, and "let’s face it, if W.E. weren’t directed by Madonna, it would have received a far kinder response in Venice." And he also points up that an "aggressively snotty" reaction by British press "might be expected for a film by an American interloper about the Wallis Simpson/Edward VIII affair."

 

"It’s perhaps alarming to see how a handful of Venice reviews has started an avalanche of declarations around the world that the film is a disaster even before it’s screened anywhere else," adds Goodridge. "The response it got, certainly by the Brits, was perhaps pre-ordained."

 

The journalist also points up that "the media loves to tear down" music stars "attending to make a splash in the movie business" proclaming them as "pretenders." But, is not enough that "Madonna has acted in films by Warren Beatty, Alan Parker, Woody Allen, John Schlesinger and Abel Ferrara?"

 

Anyway, the film critic thinks that Madonna's W.E. is "far from dead in the water, and real audiences might actually enjoy it." "That surprise factor – “it’s not so bad after all” – could help the film pick up some steam commercially when it opens to paying audiences."

 

In fact, Mike Goodrigde thinks that the movie, even though it is not a masterpiece, has "some impressive work" and "some of the performances are strong, the production is fine and there is genuine emotion in the finale." But, most of all, "it’s a film of grand ambition and considerable risk, rare commodities in any movies these days."
