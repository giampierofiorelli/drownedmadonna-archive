Madonna saves her father's vineyard

Like a vintage

Corker ... Madge pours cash into vineyard

BIG-HEARTED MADONNA has uncorked some of her vast wealth to save her dad’s struggling wine business.

The singer has stumped up a fruity half a million pounds to prevent Ciccone Vineyard closing down — because hubby GUY RITCHIE loves their wine so much.

Guy is mad on Tony Ciccone’s 2002 Dolcetto and Pinot Noir wine, so he persuaded his mega-rich wife to inject cash into the vineyard.

A pal of the singer, who reached No3 in 1984 with Like A Virgin, said: “Madonna hasn’t always had the best relationship with her father but she is very close with him now.

“When she learned the company wasn’t doing so well she decided she wanted to step into the fray.

“But Tony is a very proud man and didn’t want to accept charity, so he made her a major shareholder in Ciccone Vineyard. She was a little reluctant at the start but Guy is a huge fan of the Pinot Noir — Tony has been sending them bottles at Christmas for years.

“He couldn’t bear to be without it, so he convinced Madonna to put some money in.”

The Ciccones have a huge vineyard in the Leelanau Peninsula, Michigan, in the States.

Madonna has always kept her distance from the company, but Tony approached his daughter when it started going through a lean patch.

Pals of the star say she was dithering on the deal until wine-lover Guy convinced her.

She then wrote a cheque for nearly half a million pounds and became the major shareholder.


Out for a run ... Star with hubby Guy and trainer

The Material Girl may have bucketloads of money but at least she’s generous with it. She donates to loads of charities and gives huge amounts of her cash to the Kabbalah faith.

In fact she has just forked out £12million to set up a Kabbalah school in New York. Madge doesn’t drink much alcohol herself. Although she enjoys the odd glass of wine, her favourite tipple is Guinness.

But she’s been abstaining altogether since starting her amazing Reinvention world tour, to cope with the huge physical demands of her stage show.

Madge is currently in Britain for the tour’s UK leg and she was snapped jogging around the grounds of her Wiltshire estate with her trainer and Guy.

She was obviously keeping in top shape for her gruelling concert schedule.

Guy, on the other hand, might just be working off the effects of a session on his beloved Ciccone Dolcetto

KABBALAH EXPERTS SHUN MADONNA

contactmusic.com

Top Kabbalah experts in Israel have slammed MADONNA's attempts to follow the offshoot of Judaism - because only men and born-Jews can practice it.

The MATERIAL GIRL is planning to tour holy sites of the troubled Middle Eastern country in September (04) and celebrate the Jewish New Year there, just days after her RE-INVENTION world tour concludes.

But RABBI YITZHAK KEDURI, one of the country's most respected Kabbalist, has dashed the superstar's hopes of meeting him.

Rabbi Keduri told local newspaper MAARIV, "I don't know her, I don't know of her and I won't see her."

The religious leader added the study of Kabbalah was not open to women or non-Jews - making the singer ineligible on both conditions of her sex and the fact she was raised a Catholic.

RABBI SHLOMO RIFKIN, chief rabbi of the Efrat settlement in the southern West Bank, explains, "Kabbalah is an added mystical tier to Judaism which comes after there is a total acceptance of a religious lifestyle and a religious value system.

"The traditional idea is that anyone studying Kabbalah must first be a mature, practicing Jew.

"Maturity is important because some people can get wrapped up in the esoteric spirituality which can sometimes be cheapened into general love fests, or debauchery.

"In the Hollywood sense, the people who are accepting Kabbalah with such alacrity, such as Madonna, are taking many of the mystical concepts but not necessarily the unique lifestyle."

While Rabbi Rifkin acknowledges Madonna may be seeking a more meaningful life through the Kabbalah, he does see evidence of it in her sexy image.

Rabbi Rifkin adds, "As an actress and a singer, if the quality of her shows has changed fundamentally since she's found Kabbalah, and if sex is not paraded publicly then I would say its positive.

"If it hasn't influenced these things, then it's just a Hollywood fad that is meaningless in terms of Judaism."

Madonna has been studying the Kabbalah since 1997 and has converted her director husband GUY RITCHIE and introduced her famous pal BRITNEY SPEARS, who has demonstrated an on/off interest it.

In the past year (begs03) DEMI MOORE, ASHTON KUTCHER, LINDSAY LOHAN, WILMER VALDERRAMA and PARIS HILTON have all expressed varying degrees of interest in the Kabbalah.

Tor owner using Madonna verdict.
A farmer who closed a Dartmoor landmark is to follow the example of the pop star Madonna in trying to keep it shut.

Mary Alford is using the Human Rights Act to attempt to deny walkers access to Vixen Tor at Merrivale. It is due to be re-opened under the Right to Roam.

The Tor was closed to the public in May 2003 after it was bought by Ms Alford.

In June, Madonna partly won a bid to stop ramblers using part of her estate on the Wiltshire/Dorset border, saying her human rights would be infringed.

Vixen Tor is private land, but for more than 30 years access was allowed by its previous owner.

However, when the land was bought by farmer Mary Alford, she said she was advised to close it by her insurers as she could be liable if anyone was injured while walking or climbing.

The Tor was then included on official maps showing areas open to the public under Right to Roam laws.

But Mrs Alford is appealing against that, citing the defence used by Madonna.

The singer claimed 142 hectares of land of her £9m estate at Ashcombe House, on the Wiltshire/Dorset borders, had been wrongly classified as "open country".

She and husband Guy Ritchie succeeded in keeping part of the estate closed arguing walkers would spoil the peaceful enjoyment of their property.

However, John Bainbridge of the Dartmoor Preservation Association, said: "To buy a piece of land which you know the public wanders round and then to try and close it on human rights grounds is a farce."

Like a Pilgrim
MADONNA is so wrapped up in her Kabbalah-blah-blah studies that she is taking an “Energy Tour” to Israel next month.

Madge believes the weird pilgrimage will enable her to go back in time, undo any negative actions and fuel up on energy from the “upper worlds”

It sounds like a load of old baloney to me.

Most people want to relax, catch some sun and party on their annual vacation.

But not the Kabbalah-obsessed Queen Of Pop.

Immediately after her Re-invention Tour finishes in Europe, Madonna — or Esther as she’s known within the religion — will join hundreds of other red-bracelet wearing Kabbalists on the spiritual journey.

Madge has forked out more than £20,000 for herself, husband GUY RITCHIE, their two kids LOLA and ROCCO and a few staff to go on the Kabbalah magical mystical tour.

The journey is taking place in mid-September during the holiest time of the Jewish calendar — a special occasion for all Jews around the world.

But the specifics of Madonna’s trip sound like mumbo jumbo.

The first section of the pilgrimage — centred on the holy day of Rosh Hashanah on September 15 — offers a chance to go back in time to undo any bad actions.

A Kabbalah advert says: “Every word, thought and action you put out there creates positive or negative effects. And like boomerangs, they come back to you.

“You have the chance to go back in time and delete all the negative actions.

“Once your slate is clean, you have the opportunity to restart your life.”

Then on Yom Kippur or Day Of Atonement on September 24, the pilgrims will: “Walk into the immaterial bank which exists in the upper world and grab all the energy you’ll need to truly manifest the life you want.”

I’m sure Madge believes it’s all very important — but it sure doesn’t sound like much fun for the rest of the family.

Madonna has clearly forgotten the chorus to her own hit single Holiday where she urged us to celebrate — not meditate.

Me and Madge: the untold story.
From Times:

Years ago a lonely Rod Liddle made a pass at a strange blonde in a bar. Big mistake

Madonna, like the poor, is always with us. We are forever taunted with rumours that she is thinking of leaving Britain because the weather is so awful. She’s off to Los Angeles where the sun shines all day long in a civilised, American, manner. We read all this in the newspapers and then we silently pray: God, please let it rain, or snow, continually for the next seven weeks. Let it stop only when she has kicked off her shoes and ordered her first mineral water as the plane touches 15,000ft and it is too late to turn around.

But she never, in the end, goes, does she? What have we done to deserve the woman? Her latest incarnation — as a faux-English country landowner whining at ramblers to get orrf moi laand — grates even more than her musical career. But at least one can understand her pique at having people such as Janet Street-Porter stamping around within sight of her and jabbering about the right to roam. Her musical career, though — well, I just don’t get it.

But hang on, let me declare an interest here — I always have to when I’m talking about this benighted woman. Thing is, about ten years ago, I made a pass at her in the lobby of an American hotel and it was rebuffed. Actually it wasn’t merely rebuffed; she looked at me as if I had defecated in front of her. I didn’t know it was Madonna. I just saw this blonde bint standing by herself in the lobby of a hotel and, gripped by loneliness and libido, inquired if she fancied a quick drink in the bar. She did not even reply, she just looked. I wish I could express in words what the look conveyed. But believe me, I bear no grudge. I went down to the bar and had a drink instead with a middle-aged businessman from Des Moines. He was cool.

Maybe it’s a blind spot peculiar to me, but I do not understand Madonna’s popularity or her longevity. There are plenty of other things I don’t understand — geometry, the Lorenz Equations, why people keep cats, the Conservative Party policy on Iraq, to name but four — but of all of them I find Madonna’s success most difficult to fathom.

She is about to launch herself on us again, with a new tour. There will be those largely dismal hits belted out to a pristine, soulless, backing track or pick-up band and I daresay something mildly sexually provocative in her stage act. Perhaps she’ll fellate a giant chicken this time: she’s done men and an Evian bottle before, of course. And the music critics will give her lukewarm reviews, as they almost always do and yet later they’ll forget that she wasn’t actually that good and write of her as being the most brilliant female pop performer ever, ever, someone who has changed the course of music and — this is the bit I really don’t get — empowered women in the process. Madonna has empowered women? She’s about as empowering as a burka.

The root of the puzzle lies in the very qualities for which she is praised. It is almost never about the music. Few would seriously advance a case for a Madonna single gracing the top 500 songs of the past 20 years: they mostly flee the memory as soon as they are heard. They are, for the most part, the aural equivalent of McDonald’s chicken nuggets. I remember watching her perform (the woman-empowering) Like a Virgin 21 years ago on Top of the Pops and feeling rather disgusted with myself, as if I had been caught perusing a copy of Reader’s Digest. Surely nobody is going to be taken in by that, I thought to myself at the time, as she pouted and pranced and simpered prettily in lacy Topshop ur-chic. But then, I also thought Labour would win the election of that year. In the prediction stakes, I make William Rees-Mogg seem like Nostradamus.

Perhaps two or three of Madonna’s singles can be listened to more than once without inducing terminal ennui. The aforesaid Like a Virgin, the harmlessly bouncy Into the Groove and more recently the first six bars of Don’t Tell Me, ie, before she starts her caterwauling. Set beside this meagre list are some quite horrendous crimes against good taste: American Pie — perhaps the worst cover version ever recorded, worse even than the J. Geils Band’s massacre of Where did Our Love Go. The “hilarious” collaboration with Ali G which gave us Music. The, uh, not very woman-empowering Papa Don’t Preach. The endless, inane, Holiday. Low-stress funk work-outs which would shame the Sugababes. Vapid FM pop shorn of the stuff which makes pop music fun — spontaneity and wit.

But, as I say, few make claims for Madonna’s musical talents: you rarely read a review praising her to the skies. The claims for her brilliance are rather more mysterious and relate to stuff which you don’t immediately hear when you put on a Madonna album. We are told to adore her because she has single-mindedly controlled her own career, as if this were unusual in a woman — hey, talk to Joni Mitchell, Nina Simone or Polly Harvey, huh?

In fact, she was one of the first pop performers to view the creative process of making music precisely as if it were a “normal” career, as if she were climbing the corporate promotional ladder in a very large insurance company. Almost every stunt she has pulled in the past 20 or so years has been designed to accord with a shift in public taste — ie, it has been done to make money, usually through applying the principle of the lowest common denominator — rather than through any desire to achieve genuine artistic merit. Today, I suppose, such a thing is par for the course, and to think of pop music in any other terms is hopelessly romantic. When interviewed these days, bands will at some point start talking about their “career progression” — as if they were middle managers shortly to embark on a training course at a conference centre in Hertfordshire. I find it all a little depressing, frankly, and I suppose Madonna is at least partly to blame.

But it is not true that she was the first woman to control her own career and nor do I see how, through her disco-lite output, and sexual pandering and panting, she has done anything to empower women. Of course, she tells us that she has. But then, she would, wouldn’t she? In fact, this notion of control, if viewed in any other performer — such as Prince, for example — would be seen as incipient megalomania. I can see that it has paid off handsomely for Madonna, but I don ’t see why we should revere her for it.

And every so often she decides, with crushing premeditation and calculation, to “push the boundaries” in some way or another. So, she takes some of her clothes off for a coffeetable book about sex, the photographs a strange (and strangely cold) hybrid of Robert Mapplethorpe, Bill Brandt and Razzle — ie, stuff we’ve all seen many times before and done better before. Or she’ll kiss a woman on stage — Lordy, ’pon mah soul, the girl be a lesbian! Or swear a bit — fuck me! And boundaries are hardly pushed at all, really. It’s simply a case of expectations being met. Pretty low expectations, too.

Her new tour will be lapped up, I reckon — almost entirely by women. The strange thing is that despite her obvious attractiveness, Madonna does not possess legions of heterosexual male fans. The horrible suspicion occurs that Madonna is how a lot of women would like to be.
