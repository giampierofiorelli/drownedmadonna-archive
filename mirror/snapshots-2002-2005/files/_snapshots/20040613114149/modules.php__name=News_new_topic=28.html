2004 fashion awards of Good Housekeeping magazine
Madonna's house in order

by Phil Kemp


It's that time of year again.

The time for the big one, the most accurate reading on the cultural barometer.

Yes, it's the 2004 fashion awards of Good Housekeeping magazine.

Comes round so soon, doesn't it?

And the winner is, in the which-lady-scrubs-up-best-even-though-she's-over-40-and-American-and-this-is-Britain category, is...Madonna.

Bet Mrs Ritchie'll treat herself to another four-hour, high-octane session of Kabbalah brain-elevating transcendentalism or something to celebrate.


A honcho from the supermarket rag explained to the Times before we had time to switch off and read something else: "The great thing about Madonna is that she constantly looks good and constantly re-invents herself and sets trends."

Well spotted.

High among the tut-tuts and finger-wags dished out by GH pollsters goes to bits-showing ballsy bawler, Christina Aguilera, who, falls into the "should know better" fashion category, along with the odious Trinny and Susannah of sneery-snooty-telly-fash fame, plus Nigella, Meg 'remember her?' Mathews, Sven's Nancy and Linda 'Snip Snip' Barker, the stencil-happy flesh-flasher of the D-list DIY scene.

Tomorrow, June Whitfield and Des O'Connor duet on the Acker Bilk remix of Lady Marmalade at the People's Friend Awards, Poppe Inne Tearooms, Swanage.

$30,000 US in gifts await Grammy presenters, performers.
A bag full of about 45 "thank you" goodies worth up to $30,000 awaits Grammy performers and presenters this weekend.

The items range from low-ticket products like teeth whitening strips to pricey plastic surgery and laser eye treatments. Arranged in the green room of the Staples Centre - which is decked out to look like a comfy home complete with a queen-sized bed - stars are invited to poke around and fill a plastic bag with their favourites.

"Celebrities need to be treated as such and when they come to award shows they don't get paid," explains Lash Fary, president of Distinctive Assets, the company that has been assembling the grab bags for the Grammy Awards for the last five years. "The thank you gifts that you get have become very lavish, intricate affairs."

But do celebrities like Madonna, Celine Dion and Christina Aguilera, who already make far more cash than the average person, really need free stuff?

"A gift is never about need. It's about thanking people," says Fary.

Besides, he adds, companies don't mind forking over hundreds of dollars worth of merchandise since having your product in the hands of A-list stars usually boosts sales.

Madonna, Beck Among Grammy Performance Presenters.

Similar to last year's "host-less" Grammy Awards ceremony, the 2004 event will utilize music, film and television personalities to introduce performance segments of the show. Madonna, Beck, Queen Latifah and Evanescence singer Amy Lee have been tapped to represent the world of music during Sunday's (Feb. 10) CBS broadcast from the Staples Center in Los Angeles.

Also on hand will be actors Marg Helgenberger, Samuel L. Jackson, Cuba Gooding Jr. and Sarah Jessica Parker, as well as actor/comedians Jack Black and George Lopez and comedian/talk show host Ellen DeGeneres.

Not on the list of confirmed participants at deadline is Janet Jackson, who, along with Justin Timberlake, put CBS in hot water with the now infamous breast-baring finale to the MTV-produced Super Bowl XXXVIII halftime show. Although never officially confirmed by the Recording Academy, which stages the Grammys, Jackson was thought to be introducing a tribute to Luther Vandross involving Celine Dion, Alicia Keys and Richard Marx.

A call to Grammy representatives was not immediately returned.

Timberlake, on the other hand, is still listed among the show's performers. He is part of a bill that also includes Sting, Dave Matthews, Vince Gill and the Neptunes' Pharrell Williams in a tribute to the 40th anniversary of the Beatles' first U.S. television appearance on "The Ed Sullivan Show" and a funk tribute with Robert Randolph & the Family Band, Earth, Wind & Fire, OutKast and Parliament Funkadelic.

Also set to perform are 50 Cent, Foo Fighters and Chick Corea, the White Stripes, Martina McBride, Christina Aguilera, Beyoncé, Black Eyed Peas, Jackson Browne, Emmylou Harris, Alicia Keys, Martina McBride, Prince, the Eagles' Timothy B. Schmit, Sean Paul, Billy Bob Thornton and Dwight Yoakam.

Madonna scores lifetime achievement award at NRJ
Dido Scores in France as Madonna and Britney Look On
Sat January 24, 2004 06:07 PM ET

CANNES, France (Reuters) - Dido on Saturday added two awards to a bulging trophy case, scoring best international female artist and international artist of the year at France's biggest music awards, hosted by European radio group NRJ.

"It's all a bit too good to be true. I've had a great time tonight," the notoriously publicity-shy singer said upon receiving her awards.

The night's biggest winner was French rock group Kyo, who cleaned up four awards, including best French album, French song and French group of the year.

The award show got off to a hip-shaking start when Britney Spears, fresh from her rapid-fire marriage/divorce in Las Vegas this month, performed a crotch-grabbing rendition of her new single "Toxic."

Moments later, Britney met Madonna on stage -- their first joint appearance since sharing an infamous public kiss in August.

Britney presented Madonna with an award commemorating her hits-filled career. Clutching the award, Madonna said: "My ambition may be American, and I may have married a Brit, but my heart belongs to France."

Christina Aguilera, who was also at the music awards, said she regretted not sharing in the kiss between Madonna and Britney in August.

"I would have been open and up for anything, but the music wouldn't allow it," she told reporters backstage.

Other winners on Saturday night included goth-inspired rockers Evanescence, who claimed international breakthrough artists of the year; Justin Timberlake, who scored best international male artist; and Blue and Elton John, who took the prize for international song of the year for "Sorry Seems to be the Hardest Word."
