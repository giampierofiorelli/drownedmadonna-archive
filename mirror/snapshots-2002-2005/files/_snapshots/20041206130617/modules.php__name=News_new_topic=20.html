What an incredible experience in Holland!

Yesterday, our friend Jorge (aka jor-g) from Spain sent to us some photos he took in Arnhem (Holland) for the re-Invention Tour.

We were too busy due to the Vogue backdrop video for the re-Invention Tour to post them... but finally we can show the pics, as well as Jorge story.

"We are from Spain, and decided to go to Holland to see the shows, a country we’d never been before and wanted to visit. We were 10 days there, and visited cities as Amsterdam, Rotterdam, La Haya.. and of course Arnhem! It was a very good idea. We found there quiet and beautiful people. We were front row, and could see the show and Madonna better than we could have ever imagined. We had Madonna so close that we went mad, and enjoyed the concert at its best.

Here I enclose some pictures we did:


The Gelredome in Arnhem.


An advertisement near the Gelredome. A huge one, there were several like this near the stadium.


Jorge in the streets of Amsterdam, beside another advertisement.


Jorge, David and Bruno waiting before the show. Only a few hours waiting, and any push. It was great!

 
Jorge front row. We got the best place to see the show!


Photos from 8th September in Arnhem, made by Bruno. These are not very good pictures (he used a very bad disposable camera, with no zoom). Security was very high in this show, and all cameras seen front line were requisitioned. So he had to hide his camera behind of me, and he couln’t use the flash either, so the pictures are dark.

 
A picture of the stage once the show finished.


Madonna made front page in every local newspaper the following day. Here David and Bruno with 2 of them.

10 - Jorge and Arjan again in Arnhem, beside another advertisement.


Elena, Jorge and Sara in our own spanish after party!!


We had a experience in Holland we’ll remember for the rest of our lives. Thanks to our friends there, Arjan and Gé!! You are so great. Also to Johan and his colleagues, and all the people we meet.

Can’t wait to see the show again in the official DVD release and documentary."

Slane Castle Memories.
From our reader Erich from Dublin (Ireland):

It's Tuesday, I'm sitting in to right this during lunch hour even though it's sunny and pleasant here in placid life Monkstown. I may well be chosing to sit in, to tell all, rather than crank my limbs into some working state and take my usual lunchtime walk. If I were to talk like the media and reviews and radio 'moan'shows I'd interject with woes of cramped legs, swollen laryx, lead-arched back, itchy tired sleep-deprived eyes and general weariness. BUT I WON'T...because I - me - yours truely just experienced the most envigorating, life pulsating, dream injected reality I could ever have imagined, wished for or fretted over. I went to see Madonna. I did the do, Slaned at Slane, rocked the castle; in short I wrapped up all the emotions and teen to present (yeah old ok!) excitement that a Madonna record has ever given me and chewed and swallowed and sang and chopped every single second that she was on stage - and relived those moments like they were my last. I can't imagine it as being better - were I to have been arm reaching the stage or dripped on in the pit within the stage by the woman herslf. I shake as I write. I'm in love, promoted, high, orgasmic, reborn. :0)

Need you hear more? This is all mine, I know all who went to Slane with me felt varying degrees of enjoyment, some overshadowed by the LONG WALK home. Did it matter to me? I was in total exhausted shock when it ended, standing still in the throngs for two hours pretty much just passed me by, allowed me to catch my breath, breathe; call it back into my memory. I should apologise to those around me (who fear me now - the lunatic I became), but I know that there is no need. It was a time we forgive all sorts of behaviour, especially joy. Jumping joy.

We went to Slane in a mini bus and took no time to get there, arriving on the downward slope at about 1.30ish. The gates were not to open until 3.30 but I not sure when they started letting us through. We were happy, camped out on the side of the road, the crowd growing with each threatened shower. The hard n trendy type we were, we refused (I think it was a silent dare) to buy poxy poncos form the varying sellers along the 2k trk from where we disembarked. I had organised a moxy of plain n simple (ok - a touch of gourmet haha) sandwiches for the masses and yes, I was tempted to set up a stall for the laugh!..The boys had kindly slapped them together in my place before leaving as I was bouncing off the walls and unable to communicate nor coordinate anything other than a "1-2-3 breath 1-2-3 breath". It was difficult. Who knew I was such a fan?!

Outside Slane we were hit with the first shower and took cover under the plastic bags - extra tough bin liners - green to coordinate with the 'in the field' theme lol, and dried ourselves pleasantly in the sun that followed, swigging a wee winter/summer warmer of ruby red grapefruit juice with a dash of vodka. Warmed the toes I tell ya :0) When the crowd started to shift we hitched up and took the stoney trek through the gates of Slane Castle. Had I known we were going on a treasure hunt to find the Castle we could have made a game of it! It didn't take too long to walk again 2k and without security checks we found ourselves skipping dwonward toward s the stage. What a sight! The stage didnt look to much to start with but I expected that. You don't show all your wears at once my mammy thought me...

We found a spot no more the 10-15metres from the front to pitch up or down again. Sambos, beer and chips warmed us throught the next shower - the rain really didnt bother anyone, the form was great and we were a good ole bunch of people. Good people. Nice people!! I went walk about a couple of times, with Philip ,with James, then Eoin, James again and bumped into lots of nice, different and interesting people (ala Kat & Kim) hahahahaa. We had a couple of beers without over indulging and as Philip said - it felt very surreal like..is this really happening! Paul Okokeycookey came on and played a disjointed set, didn't matter cause I headed for a walkabout again. Afterward Iggy (isn't he dead) Pop played, roared, practically begged for attention through his slot. It wasn't impressive but I'm happy to say I'm not a rocker so what would I know? I was enjoying the chat though so...

It lashed rain again once he left the stage - pity - don't rockers usually look like the need a wash? lol, time enough for another toilet run before it was all to begin. We knew an hour wait ensued from UK reviews, which was perfect as the last heavy shower of the day fell, giving the stage hands barely enough time to dry the set. The opening was a brilliant build up, it was Madonna reading scripts (think it is off her Like a Prayer 12" RECORD hahah). The imagery was really strong, arty and freaky...which was the only arty bit of the show -off course folks this is all from my little perspective. I warmed up the ole throat a few times with chirpy little roars and braced myself lest I should faint when she came on stage. It opened, the vibes to Vogue began. Madonna appeared in high heeled boots in an apparent yoga move - looked way to sexy for that. The was a little skirmish when she noticed wet bits on the stage(off course the papers tied her reaction in with the control-crap they've been hyping on about for ages).

The song list..I can't go into it..it'll take to long but I will tell you my favourites. My first was Burning Up. A true oldie from her first album, totally rejeuvinated and stripped back to a rock style guitar playing Madge. I loved it and Iggy Pop would have been proud of my head banging - not sure about the wee girl in front of me who got bashed a bit lol, I apologised. She didn't care, I think my reaction actually gave herself and friend and probably half the crowd two metres diametre a bit of an extra buzz if I do say so myslef ahahaa...I loved it. When she did similar to Material Girl I took my forst look back and saw the whole crowd up the hill going nuts..do NOT believe the reports - the crowd were loving it.

Madonna had loads of interaction with the crowd, again despite what I've heard on the radio and from others. Not only did she say hi to us lol, she joked and LAUGHED about being cold, "rain rain go away" - she screamed about her first visit here, dedicated a SUPERB rendition of Crazy For You to her fans who've stuck by her etc etc etc! and if I could have mustered up a sad ole tear I would have, but I was so happy I couldn't. She thnaked us for being such an incredible audience and she had FUN! You could see it on the giant screens. I mean for her..the view of the castle and the full moon hanging plump in the sky..the crowd dancing and singing loud loud to every song. It must have been for her - incredible. I defy any of ya to disagree lol.

I loved the fact she also sang lots of songs from American Life, did a great version of Deeper and Deeper, a total mind 'n heart pulling version an Evita song, the bit where she dies in the movie. Her voice has never been better, I've heard it before and I've seen the dvds etc of her other tours. She mimed to two songs I think, Vogue and Die Another Day. Again in the papers she was criticised for singing Imagine. The John Lennon song. She sang it very simply, without doopdeedoing and changing the framework of the song, yet it seemed totally her own. Again, I was blown away with it. All the hands joined and swayed and people felt good. I felt good!

Hit after hit as they say lashed through and I howled till it hurt, jumping like a lunatic and probably making all the boys behind me who'd know my face (and being a Madonna concert - familiarity was pretty much expected) think me crazy, loop the loop or totally whacked on something illegal. lol. I was having such fun that when she broke into Holiday I thought to myself - why is she singing this half way through the show! It didn't occur to me it was about to end..really! When it ended I knew there would be no encore but that was ok, I had expended every last ounce of energy I had been building for weeks.

The production so seamless and flowing that there was no lull, her voice and performance was fantastic, she enjoyed herself - you could see it, the preaching and teaching wasn't anything as in your face as the papers had reported, yes there was the war sequence, yes during Imagine there was strong imagery which would stir the senses, gone were the baltant sexal overtones from all of her earlier tours and that was replaced with a hyper sexy woman. She looked stunning in a trouser suit with hat singing Nothing Fails. It was beautiful. Reminded me of a wee Ozzie fella I was a little nuts over, but hey - didn't bring me down. Everysong she sang made me feel good. I sound stupid but it did. Every song brought me back to where I first heard it, what it meant and it felt something beyond emotion to sing it along with her.

I never knew I could be a 'number one' fan.

I was.
