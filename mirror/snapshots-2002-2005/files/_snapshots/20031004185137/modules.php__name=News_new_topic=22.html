''The English Roses'' selling well in the UK.
Madonna's children's book, "The English Roses" (Puffin), has sold (in the UK) 14,494 copies in the two weeks since publication. That is about 138,000 copies fewer than David Beckham's "My Side" (HarperCollins) has sold in the same period, and 3,000 fewer than the bestselling hardback novel, Robert Harris's "Pompeii" (Hutchinson). But it is a very high number for a children's picture book.

Nicholas Clee from "The Bookseller."

''The English Roses'' signing in New York.
Yesterday Madonna did an in store appearance to promote her new children's book, "The English Roses." during a book signing at Barnes & Noble bookstore in New York City, Madonna read her book to a group of small children and then handed out signed copies to the 250 winners of a lottery held by the book store. While she was reading "The English Roses," Jeremy Zorek, 18 months, of New York, walks to the front. You can see him posing with Madonna.
Outside, a crowd of lottery losers crowded near the front door hoping to catch a glimpse of Madonna.
Madonna said writing "The English Roses," was "tremendously liberating. I never felt the pressure of having to impress anybody," she said yesterday at the book signing in midtown Manhattan.
"In the first book, the lesson is about how destructive feelings of envy and jealousy are. And everybody feels it, not just little girls," said Madonna.
Madonna said people should not be surprised that she has tackled children's books as a project. "I have children and I read to them every night, so it's a perfect extension of who I am," said Madonna, "I think children need to read inspiring books."

London launch party of ''The English Roses''.
Madonna has arrived at the launch party for her first children's book "The English Roses", at the Roof Gardens, London. It was a tea party. About 100 children sat eagerly at her feet in a London roof garden as she read from her illustrated tale.
Madonna, flanked by her son, Rocco, and her daughter, Lourdes, sat on a garden swing chair reading in her best bedtime story voice.
Before launching into her brief reading, the icon who is hounded by paparazzi and tabloids around the world confessed to the children: "I like little kids better than big people because they don't have any bad habits yet." "I think I am being upstaged by my children," she said as Rocco fidgeted in the seat beside her and Lourdes beamed with pride. Madonna got the biggest cheer from the children when she showed them one of the illustrations -- a picture of kids who were literally colored green with envy. Artists Add New Voice To Children's Books.
Seeking a crossover of a different kind, top recording artists Madonna and LeAnn Rimes are about to invade the children's book market. "Artists are starting to realize how important the children's audience is," says Stephanie Owens Lurie, president/publisher of Dutton Children's Books, which on Aug. 25 is releasing "Jag," the first book by country/pop artist Rimes. "The children's audience has a lot of spending money and influence over what their parents buy for them."

Madonna's first children's book, "The English Roses," due Sept. 15, is expected to be published in 42 different languages in more than 100 countries. It is said to be the widest simultaneous multi-language release ever for a book. "We're going to have a new generation of children who may very well think of Madonna as a children's book author first," says Nicholas Callaway, editor/publisher of Callaway Editions.

Madonna's work will be released by various publishers around the world. The Penguin Group has secured the English-language rights; Callaway will handle publishing of the books in the U.S., with Penguin as the distributor. Callaway Editions is projecting that the first U.S. printing of the 48-page "The English Roses" will exceed 400,000 copies.

To build anticipation for "The English Roses," the plot and the identities of the characters will not be revealed until the title is published. Callaway says the books were Madonna's idea and that she will author them herself without ghost writers. He adds that Madonna will donate her publishing proceeds to charity.

A multilayered international marketing blitz is planned for "The English Roses." Gap Kids will dedicate about 500 of its retail-store windows to the book and will give limited-edition tote bags to customers who buy the title along with $75 worth of Gap merchandise (Madonna has a Gap endorsement deal).

In addition, Madonna will attend launch parties Sept. 14 in London and Sept. 15 in Paris. She is scheduled to do a reading of the book Sept. 16 on an as-yet-unannounced U.S. TV broadcast.

As for Rimes, she will also enjoy a significant promotion campaign for "Jag," which she co-wrote with her husband, Dean Sheremet. Media exposure includes CBS programs "The Early Show" and "48 Hours," VH1 and ABC Radio. On Aug. 25, Rimes will appear on NBC's "Today" and sign books at Barnes & Noble's Fifth Avenue location in New York.

"Jag" is about a young female jaguar dealing with issues of friendship and insecurity. "The story has a lot to do with my experiences growing up," Rimes tells Billboard. "I was picked on by other kids at school, and I think a lot of kids are dealing with those life experiences right now. The message of the book is be yourself, conquer your fears and stand up for yourself in a good way."

Rimes has inked a deal with Dutton, a division of Penguin Young Readers Group, to write a sequel to "Jag." The sequel, which has the working title, "Jag's New Friend," is scheduled for publication in 2004. Rimes says that she and her husband have developed about five story ideas for other books. "I'm writing children's books because I wanted to connect with my fan base of small children, and I think this is a cool way to do it other than my music," she adds.

“The English roses”and “Mr Peabody’s apple: new details.

Here is again a lot of talking about Madonna’s second children book title, “Mr Peabody’s apple”. Drowned Madonna was one of the first to be interested in that, since April 5, announcing many details, included the plot of the first two books. Evidently, at that time, the news went unnoticed, even if it was continued by other prestigious websites. So, we decided to write again all the details about the two Madonna’s books.

As you all know, Madonna entered into a contract with Calloway publisher, to write five books dedicated to children from six to ten years of age. Fifteen countries has bought the copyright by Andrew Wylie, the greatest literary agent in the world. Wylie, has already drew up 15 contracts all over the world. In America, Madonna’ books will be distributed by Penguin, by Puffin in England and by Feltrinelli in Italy. Puffin manager Elaine McQuade claimed “The English Roses”, the first of five tales, will be published simultaneously in all the world on September 15. The second Title, “Mr Peabody’s apple”, will be into bookshops on November.

All five books, announced as precious albums, will be illustrated by a different artist. Madonna’s texts are short but characterized by a sharp moral: classical tales with strong values, expressed with a modern language and narrated by an authoritative teller.

“The English Roses” starts with a group of little girls as main characters. They start to envy a classmate of theirs, who looks luckier, because she’s beautiful, smart and above all, she’s all the teachers’ pet. The envy grows, the girls stop to talk and do homework with her, trying to isolate her. Till, with a coup de theatre, it comes to their knowledge what is it hiding behind the beautiful face of the girl. The little girl lost her mother, she is even forced to do housework and she’s in trouble in doing homework. For this reason the teachers are so kind with her. “The English Roses” has a wonderful happy ending: the little group of friends is going to welcome a new girlfriend.

More harsh the Second book, “Mr. Peabody’s apple”. Mr. Peabody is the coach of a baseball team. One day, a kid who plays inside his team, sees him going to the greengrocer, picking up an apple and not paying it. Astonished, the kid spread his coach is a thief to all of his friends. For him, it’s the explosion of a myth: his landmark is a criminal. So, he decides not to go anymore to the practises. When Mr. Peabody asks him the reason’s why he reacted that way, the little kid is going to answer telling him what he’s discovered that very day. The coach leads the kid to the greengrocer exactly where he was caught red - handed, picks up another apple while the dealer says: yeah, yeah, of course, as usual I send you the others at home.

The lesson will be so hard for the little kid, who asks the coach how to make up the charges, that covered his reputation with mud. Then the coach invites the kid to pick up a feather pillow and to break it, sprinkling its contents. After, he asks him to pick up all the feathers on the ground. To the kid, so discouraged by the undertaking, Mr. Peabody will explain how much easier will be to pick up feather by feather and not to renew his reputation by this time messed up. From the two stories, it’s so clear and beautiful the invitation to go beyond appearances, and just Madonna, Queen of appearances, urged the children to think over the situations before giving opinions on someone.
