From The Sun:

By VICTORIA NEWTON and OLIVER HARVEY

RADIO listeners can hear the Band Aid 20 single for the first time tomorrow.
The star-studded remake of 1984 charity classic Do They Know It’s Christmas? will make its debut on the nation’s airwaves in the morning.

The accompanying video will be shown for the first time just before BBC1’s 6pm news on Thursday.

It has an introduction by Madonna, who talks about the importance of helping the starving in Africa.

The Sun had an exclusive ringside seat yesterday as an army of stars gathered at North London’s Air Studios to record the track.

Among them were Will Young, Miss Dynamite, Busted, Joss Stone, Katie Melua and Daniel and Natasha Bedingfield.

Story of Single

OCTOBER 1: The Sun travels to Ethiopia on the 20th anniversary of the catastrophic famine. We see Africans queuing for food.

OCTOBER 4: We track down Live Aid girl Birhan Woldu.

OCTOBER 7: The Sun introduces Birhan to Bob Geldof and Tony Blair, then asks Bob to re-record Do They Know It's Christmas?

OCTOBER 8: The Sun's Dominic Mohan gets Coldplay, The Darkness and Travis on board.

OCTOBER 11: We contact Jamelia. She says yes.

OCTOBER 22: Dido, Keane and Katie Melua are added. Midge Ure books the studio.

NOVEMBER 2: Paul McCartney signs up.

NOVEMBER 3: Busted join.

NOVEMBER 14: Stars gather at Air Studios, North London to re-record the song.

NOVEMBER 16: New version will be on radio for first time.

NOVEMBER 18: Video with Madonna intro to be shown.

NOVEMBER 29: Do They Know It's Christmas? to go on sale.

Video-clip
''Me Against The Music'' video: More details.
In Spears' upcoming video for "Me Against the Music," Madonna lures the pop princess through a mazelike underground after-hours club, only to disappear into thin air when Britney gets close enough to touch.

According to the treatment for the video, shot last week in New York with director Paul Hunter, the pop singers discover they're reverse images of each other, from their dance moves to the way they dress. Britney wears black to Madonna's white. Britney is hipster to Madonna's old-school. Britney's section of the club is populated with cool kids, flaunting pink and purple hair while taking whiffs from an oxygen tank, while Madonna's stakes claim to a territory filled with men in suits, smoking cigars.

Though the areas are separated by a wall, somehow Britney can sense Madonna on the other side. At first, it seems like they have a psychic or telepathic connection - but then Madonna's visage starts appearing on television monitors throughout the club, making Britney more determined to find her.

As if the ESP element wasn't enough, the club proves not to be governed by the usual rules of reality in other areas, such as when Madonna finds herself a room covered with fallen leaves, where she can stand on a swing to amuse herself. But instead of wondering how the leaves got inside in the first place, she's more concerned with coaxing Britney to rid herself of her inhibitions: "Hey, Britney, you say you wanna lose control/ Sexy lady, I'd rather see you bare your soul/ If you think you're so hot, better show me what you got/ Let me see you dance."

Madonna's solo, however, turns out to be but a siren's song, as she is nowhere to be found once Britney hears the call and makes it to the swing room. Over the course of several rooms, Britney searches desperately as Madonna plays an elaborate cat-and-mouse game with her.

"Making the Video: Britney Spears" premieres October 20at 6 p.m. on MTV with a preview on Access Hollywood tonight.

Video-clip
Further details about Samuel Bourdin suing Madonna.
Just an update about Guy Bourdin's son suing Madonna.

"It's one thing to draw inspiration; it's quite another to simply plagiarize the heart and soul of my father's work," Samuel Bourdin said in a statement.
A rep for the superstar said, "Madonna hasn't been served yet, so there's no comment for now."

Jennifer Lopez recently found herself in a similar predicament with her video for "I'm Glad," an over-the-top homage to Adrian Lyne's 1983 film "Flashdance." Paramount cried foul and fired off a legal notice to Sony (parent company of Lopez's label, Epic Records) but ultimately it was a win-win for both sides. The matter was resolved out of court for an unspecified amount, label and studio both got press coverage out of the incident, and J.Lo's name was instantly linked to a much-rumored remake of "Flashdance."
