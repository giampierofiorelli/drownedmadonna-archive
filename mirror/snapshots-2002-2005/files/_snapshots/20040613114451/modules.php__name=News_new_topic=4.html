Read It First Here: Madonna to produce documentary about the 80's for NBC
Maverick, NBC tuned to the '80s
Spesh follows twentysomethings as they travel thru the decade
 
 By JOSEF ADALIAN
Oseary

 

Like, totally, fer shure: A miniseries about the 1980s is coming to NBC -- and, oh my gawd, Madonna and Guy Oseary's Maverick Films is exec producing.

Peacock has pacted with Maverick to develop a four-hour mini set in the decade of excess. Oseary, Maverick TV's Michael Rosenberg and Mark Morgan will exec produce in association with NBC Studios.

NBC longform topper Jeff Gaspin is overseeing for the network.

Madonna will be involved in the project, but whether she takes a producer credit will depend on her schedule. Singer is expected to tour within the next year.

Storyline for the mini will follow a group of twentysomethings as they travel through the decade, starting with John Lennon's assassination and ending with the fall of the Berlin Wall.

The untitled 1980s mini continues NBC's decade-at-a-time exploration of pop culture. Net scored a ratings smash with "The '60s" and also did well with "The '70s."

VH1 has found ratings success with its 10-hour unscripted miniseries "I Love the '80s" and "I Love the '80s Strikes Back."

"The '80s is the hot decade right now -- not just for those of us who came of age during the period, but for those currently coming of age as well," said Gaspin, NBC's longform exec VP.

Gaspin said landing Maverick as a producer was ideal, given Madonna's status as "the decade's premiere icon," as well as Oseary's expertise on the decade.

Oseary exec produced both volumes of the '80s-friendly soundtracks to "The Wedding Singer" as well as the just-released soundtrack for "50 First Dates." Latter disc includes island-inspired covers of a number of '80s classics.

"The '80s are a big part of my life and have served as an inspiration for so much of my work," Oseary said, adding that he felt "passionate (about being) a part of this project."

On the feature front, Maverick, a producer on MGM's "Agent Cody Banks II," is working on "Rodeo Girl" for 20th Century Fox; "Twilight" with MTV and Paramount; and "My Sassy Girl" for DreamWorks.

''US Weekly'' ad on first-name basis.
Two new television spots for "Us Weekly" by Deutsch portray how familiar and relevant celebrities have become in people's lives.

The New York agency's ads show women talking about celebrities in shorthand, just by using their first names. The work introduces "Read Us, talk Us, trust Us" as a tagline.

One of the two 15-second spots shows three women in a restaurant discussing celebrities, but not mentioning anything other than their first names. "Cameron and Justin," says one, while the others reply, "I know!" "Demi and Ashton," says another, which is also met with an "I know!" The spot ends with one woman saying, "Britney and ... " while the others look on, puzzled.

A second spot shows two women discussing a date in terms of the first names of celebrities. "It started out romantic, but then he got all 'Colin' on me," says one, referring to Colin Farrell. "After all the 'Ben' he put you through?" the other replies, a reference to Ben Affleck.

Current events shaped the ads, shot Jan. 22 at Fresh restaurant in New York's Tribeca. Karen McIntyre, associate creative director on the spot, said the original ending of the first ad was "Britney and Madonna," a reference to the fact that the two women kissed at the MTV Video Music Awards last year. But during the shoot, the ending was changed to take into account that Britney briefly married a former high school friend in Las Vegas.

(...)

The spots broke last week and are running on national cable channels, including Oxygen, Lifetime and MTV.

Special Live Broadcast from Madonna Via Satellite from Europe.
The Kabbalah Centre, the largest international organization devoted to teaching the wisdom of Kabbalah, is sponsoring an opening ceremony for Spirituality for Kids, featuring a live broadcast from Madonna this Thursday, January 8, 2004, at Chavat Ronit', Kibutz Shfaim, in Telaviv, Israel.

The event celebrates the beginning of a new initiative designed to provide children with practice tools to experience life as a great adventure of opportunities and challenges, without the obstacles. The program will be attended by parents and children, political leaders, educators, members of diplomatic corps, business representatives and artists, including Madonna, via satellite from Europe.

In addition to teaching Kabbalah, the ceremony will encourage cooperative participation in various activities such as playing, painting and singing among children, parents and individuals raised in an environment historically known for its violence and hatred. By dispelling fear, mistrust, and prejudice-the root causes of violence and conflict-teachings of the Kabbalah seek to ensure a future of peace and spiritual fulfillment.

Madonna, whose recently published book for children, Mr. Peabody's Apples, has been an active member of the Kabbalah community. Madonna's book, inspired by a nearly 300-year-old story that was told to her by her Kabbalah teacher, teaches the importance of choosing words carefully to avoid causing harm to others.

Karen Berg, Founder of the worldwide organization, Spirituality for Kids, and Chief Director of the Kabbalah Centre feels that spirituality should be introduced at a young age because, "each child has the innate right to develop and evolve into a happy human being. He/she should be free, strong and able to independently control his/her own destiny; to fulfill his/her inner potential and live a rich life governed by tolerance and human dignity."

In addition to the broadcast from Madonna in Europe, the program will also highlight other notable participants and spiritual leaders as they bless the project and the actions of The Kabbalah Centre: Doron Merdinger, Aki Avny, Sandy Barr, Yael Abkasis, Yair Maymon, Pnina Turneh, Orna Pitussi, Adi Azruni, Rotem Abuhav, Leon Rosenberg, Dalik, Orna Lynn, Rena Pushkarna, Rafi Preter, Nissim Zioni, Prof. Shulamit Kartler, Dori Dankner, Chosnia Jabarra, Abu Rukun, Elias Jabour, Safta Gamilla, Yarin Kimor, Givony, Shiek Abu Ali, Shiek Manasra.

Through studies of Kabbalah, the original spiritual wisdom that predates all religions, races and national identities, the Centre believes that peace, lasting joy and fulfillment can be achieved. Its program geared toward teaching Kabbalah to children, Spirituality for Kids, is now in its tenth year. Since the program's inception in Toronto, its community has expanded and its spiritual tools have become available to children all over the world, including the Kabbalah Children's Academies of New York and Los Angeles, which were founded on the principles of Spirituality for Kids. The results were so profound in the children who were raised with these principles, that in October of 2002 the Spirituality For Kids Foundation was formed. The Spirituality For Kids Foundation is the umbrella organization that provides the funding, research, development, support, implementation, and management for all of the spirituality for kids programs, services and products.

Madonna interviewed by ''Extra''.
