The Von Bondies has signed with Sire Records.
Detroit band The Von Bondies have recently signed a record deal with Sire Records. It was Seymour Stein, the man who gave Madonna and The Ramones their first beak in the industry, who managed to pursued them to sing the deal.

"We were offered a hell of a lot more money by a number of different labels, but when Seymour stepped in we knew it was the right thing to do in signing for Sire. Money really wasn’t an issue because we have all lived without it until now. I live in downtown Detroit and nobody has any money where I live, it’s just not an issue. All we want to do is play our music, and if we have the man who put faith in The Ramones when everybody thought they were a joke behind us- then I’m quite happy."

Drowned World Tour's Steben Sisters make their ZinZanni debut.

As if Teatro ZinZanni (like dinner theater on LSD) wasn't trippy enough, the show now has the extra element of strange. Sort of.

The Steben Sisters, who play conjoined twins on HBO's "Carnivàle," made their ZinZanni debut last week. Karyne and Sarah -- who in real life are identical twins and aren't conjoined -- are part of the new "international cast" (they're Canadian) and come with mucho experience.

They were on Madonna's Drowned World Tour in 2001, Cirque Du Soleil's "Saltimbanco," and were part of the Las Vegas "O" extravaganza. The sisters Steben, who perform on trapeze, will be part of ZinZanni's Dinner & Dreams into the beginning of April.

For additional info, check http://dreams.zinzanni.org.

Patrick Leonard producing Casey Stratton.

Patrick Leonard, who wrote and produced so many songs with Madonna, has produced Casey Stratton's debut album, "At the Edge."

The music is tuneful and sophisticated. Several of the 12 tracks -- plus a hidden bonus song -- could be released as singles. Here's a review by Jeffrey Kaczmarczyk at MLive.com.

Stratton's enigmatic, often dark lyrics -- including such titles as "Contagious," "Blood" and "Dead Sea" -- are complicated and thought-provoking.

(...)

Producer Patrick Leonard, who has worked with A-list artists from Madonna to Elton John, has crafted a straight-ahead sound with tasteful effects and few studio shenanigans.

Much adult contemporary music is drivel, but this recording is refreshingly original.

Farewell to the man who taught Evita to tango.
Ken Crosby, one of the great international spies, passed away after Christmas. Ken was possessed of a nose as prominent and sharp as Natty Bumpo’s ax and a charming and warm personality. He was an attorney and the longtime Merrill Lynch chief in Washington. He and his wife, Peggy, were popular social figures.

Ken met Peggy through his friendship with her sister, a World War II OSS agent named Aline, the Countess of Romanoness, who introduced them. Aline wrote a series of books about her adventures as a spy. The most famous was The Spy Wore Red. The countess still lives in a sumptuous estancia in the south of Spain, home to her husband’s family for nearly 1,000 years. (The Shadow visited the enormous and slightly crumbling estate, a pleasant drive up from Portugal’s Costa Del Sol, one July weekend a few years ago.)

Ken was in a small and very secret FBI spy unit formed in 1940 at the direction of FDR. It was called the Special Intelligence Service and was designed to disrupt pro-German and pro-Japanese activity in Latin America. Ken, already an FBI agent, volunteered for duty and in 1942 was assigned undercover to Buenos Aires, posing as a businessman. There he befriended and reported on Juan Peron and his wife, Eva (known to fans of Madonna as Evita), whom he taught to do the tango and perhaps other things. Forced to leave the country, Ken continued acting as a spy in Mexico City and in Montevideo, Uruguay.

After the war, he headed Merrill Lynch’s offices in Havana, Madrid (where he somehow had retained the Perons’ fat stock account, perhaps with Eva’s intercession with her jealous husband) and Paris, where he continued as an undercover U.S. asset. Ken was active to the very end, and many who knew and liked him were surprised to learn that he was 87 years old.

International Herald Tribune: Digging beneath the English rose.
They could have been English Roses inspired by Madonna’s children’s book. The audience sat around decorous tea tables, while out came a model wearing a sprinkle of flower bud pattern that looked childishly innocent. Only the skirt rising high on the rump suggested a more worldly spirit at the Frost French show.
Dirty, pretty things - the title of a hit British movie - is the theme of London Fashion Week, which closes on Thursday. With more than the requisite spoonful of sugar, designers are showing for summer 2004 clothes that are primarily pink, flower-strewn and fastened with ribbons and bows. But in the shadows of this English country garden, something more subversive and sexy is going on.
