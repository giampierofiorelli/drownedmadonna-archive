Signed Madonna cowboy hat auctioned.

A motley crew of South Florida characters and hoi polloi turned up at criminal defense lawyer Roy Black’s house for his and his wife Lea’s annual black-tie bash in support of Bay Point School, which serves at-risk youths.

The evening began with a performance for the 200 biggest donors inside Black’s 11,000-square-foot home by former Bee Gees singer and Miami Beach resident Barry Gibb and his band. Following a “Saturday Night Fever” medley by the disco star, the crowd emptied out into the Blacks’ acre-size yard. They joined the other guests outside, and everyone dined on donated delicacies from the Palm Restaurant, The Forge and Randazzo’s Little Italy.

Following dinner, an auction was held that included a signed cowboy hat from Madonna, a Janet Jackson autographed outfit, Peter Maxx artwork and a signed guitar from Sting.

About $500,000 was raised for Bay Point School, Mrs. Black said.

Bay Point is a boarding school founded in 1995 by Dr. Mary Louise Cole. It educates boys 13 and older who come to the school as court-adjudicated delinquents. According to the school, almost 90 percent of the boys go on to finish high school, attend college or find employment in a vocational career.

The Von Bondies has signed with Sire Records.
Detroit band The Von Bondies have recently signed a record deal with Sire Records. It was Seymour Stein, the man who gave Madonna and The Ramones their first beak in the industry, who managed to pursued them to sing the deal.

''Sex and the City'' captures changes brought by Madonna.
Every now and then a television series so perfectly captures the mood of a culture that it becomes more than just a hit: it becomes a sociological event — something to be studied in terms of historic patterns, analyzed in the spirit of the decade, maybe even incorporated into a college lecture or two. HBO's "Sex and the City" is one of those series.

"Sex and the City" shows women being "articulate about their own sexual needs," says Camille Paglia, author of "Sexual Personae: Art and Decadence From Nefertiti to Emily Dickinson."

Ms. Paglia says, the success of "Sex and the City" marks a defeat for the "1980's anti-porn, anti-sex wing of feminists" and a victory for "the huge wing of us pro-sex feminists who came back with a vengeance in 1990, thanks to Madonna."

New edition of Trivial Pursuit with 8 questions related to Madonna.
Trivial Pursuit, the general knowledge board game which has led to countless arguments over answers, is celebrating 20 years of being on sale in Britain.

Around six million copies of the game, with its trademark cheese-shaped pieces, have been sold since it was launched in the UK in the spring of 1984.

To mark the event, makers Parker are bringing out a new edition with questions based on the past two decades.

The rise of the celebrity over that time is represented by the content of the new game, with almost 400 references to well-known people.

They include eight questions which relate to pop superstar Madonna, five about cartoon characters "The Simpsons" but only four on Prince William and three on the Queen.

Sue Jones - brand manager at Parker - said: "It’s only natural that this 20th anniversary edition of Trivial Pursuit has more celebrity related questions. Focusing on the past 20 years, nothing has intrigued the nation more than the rise of the celebrity."
"The fact that some celebrities appear more often than others is pure coincidence."

Madonna to attend Conservatives party?
Liberal singer Madonna is considering lending her support to right-wing British political party the Conservatives, by attending its annual winter ball in London on Tuesday (3 February 2004).

The Material Girl and husband Guy Ritchie are expected to go to the Grosvenor House Hotel as guests of Guy's dad John and step-mother Shireen, who is the chairperson of the exclusive Kensington and Chelsea Conservative Association.

It's thought the celebrity couple are keen to align themselves with the opposition party after becoming annoyed with a Labour government ruling that ruled land on their $15 million (GBP9 million) Ashcombe Estate near Salisbury in Wiltshire, south England will be open to ramblers.

The 45-year-old singer hopes to quash the decision that their land falls under British Right To Roam laws.

An insider says, "There are several very high profile people tipped to come to the ball, Madonna being just one.

"It would be fantastic if she showed and we would be delighted to see her. She's a natural Conservative - an independent, hard-working self-starter.

"And she's developed an interest in country affairs since she met and married Guy and they moved to Wiltshire.

"It would undoubtedly be a sensational celebrity coup for the Conservatives, and one in the eye for Labour if Madonna lends her support to the event."

Pupils painting portraits

Talented students at a Telford school could be forgiven for getting big headed after producing these stunning self portraits.

The young artists, from Madeley Court School, produced the huge pastel portraits of themselves at workshops this week.

They were assisted by workshop leader, nationally renowned artist John Denaro.

Mr Denaro used portraits of Madonna, Tony Blair and David Beckham to demonstrate the skills involved in producing their own self portraits.

He visited the school on Monday and Tuesday to help the students with their work.

The youngsters, aged between 15 and 16, had to produce a five-foot high, head and shoulders, portrait.

And they will have the chance to show off their masterpieces to fellow students when they go on display around the school.

The finished works will form the basis of exhibitions at the school's own art gallery, the school theatre and library.

The portraits will be accredited as part of the students' GCSE coursework.

Alana Lane, who is head of the school's art department, said she was thrilled by the project.

"This was a wonderful opportunity for our students to work alongside a professional artist and use this to develop and inform their own artwork."

Artist John DeNaro, right, joined pupils. With their self portraits are, from left, Laurie Jones, 15, Janis Jackson, 16, Robert Laybourne, 15, and Tara Powell, 16.
