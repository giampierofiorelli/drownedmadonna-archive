More on legal action to try stop Madonna's concert in Slane Castle.

Legal action to try to stop the planned Madonna concert going ahead on a Sunday in Slane Castle is being considered by villagers strongly opposed to the plan.

They fear that if the concert goes ahead on a Sunday, it will lead to serious public order problems.

Resident Eddie Hudson said that he can recall the serious disturbances that took place in Slane when the last superstar, Bob Dylan, played there on a Sunday in 1984.

"Within the village are the same families as 20 years ago," stated Mr Hudson. "People don't forget these things."

Mr Hudson has an agreement dating back to 1985 that a concert would never be held on a Sunday again.

Slane village has a community of 800 people, he said. Already a petition organised by another local resident, Patricia Harding, has hundreds of signatures.

"We are not talking about policing, we are talking an invasion of hundreds of thousands of people," Mr Hudson added.

Slane Castle owner, Lord Henry Mountcharles, acknowledged yesterday that he had an agreement with certain individuals dating back to the mid-Eighties. But he suggested times had changed - that agreement had allowed for just one concert a year but there had been two concerts there one year.

Mountcharles would now be making a licence application for a concert on 29 August, the only date Madonna could play.

If a licence was refused, then the concert would be off, he said.

An official announcement on the ticket prices is due to be made in 10 days.

BREAKING NEWS: Madonna Provokes Irish Ire with Sunday Concert

DUBLIN (Reuters) - Pop diva Madonna came under fire from Christians in Ireland on Friday after it was confirmed she would play her first ever Irish concert on a Sunday.    

Madonna is due to play Slane Castle, 30 miles north of Dublin, on Sunday, August 29, the castle's owner Lord Henry Mount Charles confirmed.


He denied that Madonna, who has developed an interest in Jewish mysticism recently, had refused to play on Saturday because it is the Jewish Sabbath.


"It is the only date and day feasible," he said.


But Joe Deegan, Slane's parish priest, said the decision was insensitive in a country where 90 percent of the population is Roman Catholic.


"The Lord's Day for a lot of people around this part of the world is Sunday and it seems a bit inconsiderate and insensitive that our religious beliefs are not taken into account," he told RTE state radio on Friday.


The last time Slane Castle hosted a major concert on a Sunday -- in 1984 when Bob Dylan played -- it sparked riots.


"I knew people would stir up memories of 1984," Lord Mount Charles said.


"This year Bob Dylan is playing in Galway on Sunday, June 27. So if he can play there on a Sunday, I am puzzled why there is opposition to Madonna playing Slane on a Sunday."


Deegan said he was also concerned about the content of Madonna's notoriously raunchy stage show.


"It's very sexually provocative," he said, adding, however, that he would probably go to concert, whenever it was staged, because he enjoyed Madonna's music.

BREAKING NEWS: Slane News Rolls In

Sunday date is ‘the only option’ for Madonna’s August concert at Slane

By Elaine Keogh

MADONNA will top the bill at Slane on Sunday, August 29, not because of her religious beliefs but because of the logistics of setting up her massive show, Henry Mountcharles confirmed yesterday.

He challenged critics who claimed it will prevent local people from going to mass and local politicians who want Meath County Council to refuse a licence because it is a Sunday.

The Queen of Pop does not begin her Reinvention Tour until next month and the majority of the US and British dates have sold out.

Lord Mountcharles confirmed he has wanted Madonna, “who has icon status, is highly controversial and very interesting,” to play at Slane for a number of years and it was only in the New Year that he received confirmation.

The last Sunday concert at Slane was the infamous Bob Dylan gig in 1984 when riots dominated the headlines. It was some years before concerts resumed at the castle.

“I knew people would stir up memories of 1984. This year Bob Dylan is playing in Galway on Sunday June 27, so if he can play there on a Sunday I am puzzled why there is opposition to Madonna’s date.

“The fact is that Madonna is an extraordinary artist and this is about entertainment. It is very easy to try and knock what we have done in Slane. Where were all these people when I and others tried to have the interpretative centre for Newgrange located in Slane?”

He said he and promoters MCD will go to enormous lengths to facilitate local people including putting back the time the gates open from 12 noon to 2pm so that anyone who wants to can attend mass before the concert starts.

“I fully realise that having 80,000 people in the middle of county Meath does cause inconvenience and disruption; I do actually get grief when the concerts are held on a Saturday.”

There has been wide speculation and rumour that Madonna would not play on a Saturday but Lord Mountcharles said the concert is on a Sunday because it is the only date feasible.

“Madonna plays six dates in London beforehand, the final one is Wembley on the 26th so it would not be possible logistically to get a show ready for Slane on Saturday the 28th,” he said.

Ticket details have yet to be finalised but are expected by the time MCD make a formal application for a licence for the event to Meath County Council.

Source: The Irish Examiner.com

Madonna at Slane Casle: The neverending story part 2.

Reports that Madonna wants to stage a Sunday rock concert at Slane Castle have angered Meath County Council members who demanded yesterday that officials should not grant a licence for the Augst 29 performance.

Senior Meath council official Kevin Stewart informed members that no application had yet been received from concert promoter MCD Productions for planning permission for a concert in Slane.

However, he said the promoters had informed the council that Sunday, August 29, was the proposed date for this year's concert.

Fianna Fail Councillor Hugh Gough led the opposition to the granting of a licence for a Sunday concert. He said there was huge opposition in the village of Slane to any proposal for a concert at the famous castle venue on a Sunday, largely due to rioting and disorder during the last Sunday concert in 1984 when Bob Dylan topped the bill.

Villagers are also said to be concerned that they would not be able to get to Mass if the concert went ahead at the castle on a Sunday.

Read It First Here: Mother of Reinvention.
Today the Australian "Sidney Morning Herald" has published a long article about Madonna touring this summer. We suggest you to read this analysis, which also features a comparison between Madonna and Britney Spears, respectively the queen and the would-be princess. 

The material girl has silenced critics by doing what she does best. But, asks Brett Thomas, how long will it last?

There would have been quite a sigh of relief coming from Camp Madonna last week as the magic words "sold out" quickly started appearing on a number of shows on her planned re-Invention world tour.

The relief will be partly because these concerts, her first since 2001, are an expensive night out, with top-tier tickets upwards of $US300 ($390). But mainly because, at 45, the British-based pop icon seems again to be winning her ongoing battle to remain relevant.

Never mind that Madonna's world is a tiny place that includes only a few North American cities, a few in Europe and - bizarrely - Israel (there are unlikely to be any Australian dates), this tour has already emerged as the hot ticket for 2004, despite the live competition from allegedly hotter, hipper female talent such as Britney Spears, Pink, Beyonce, Alicia Keys and Missy Elliott.

The mounting interest is also contrary to the reception given by a once adoring public to Madonna's most recent creative forays. First there was the movie disaster Swept Away, then the underperforming and much criticised American Life album and, most recently, the release of the first in a series of children's books which has done little for the author's hip factor.

Meanwhile, Madonna's finely honed ability to generate genuine shock seems to have deserted her. The banned film clip for American Life, with its anti-war and anti-George Bush imagery, was a tired, unoriginal gimmick and the manufactured controversy of the Spears kiss reeked of sad desperation, an unseemly attempt to reconnect herself with younger pop fans via the trembling lips of a classic Madonna wannabe.

But in announcing the re-Invention tour, Madonna has finally decided to play to her strengths. In the increasingly crowded and youthful world of divas and pop tarts, Madonna, whether she likes it or not, has emerged as the proven, dependable old-school talent, the Mick Jagger of the Britney and Christina set.

She has acknowledged as much by embarking on what is essentially a greatest-hits tour, with all the old favourites to be pulled out of mothballs for an older audience more interested in her best-of albums than her more recent attempts to produce contemporary, cutting-edge music.

Of course, Madonna has spent a career shaking things up, and the word re-Invention suggests she won't be pulling on those clunky old crucifixes for note-perfect versions of Like A Virgin and Lucky Star.

Indeed, she has already hired Calum "Spud" Fraser, the piper who played at her 2000 wedding to Guy Ritchie and the christening of their son Rocco, to accompany her on the five-month jaunt. He told reporters he was preparing a special bagpipe version of his favourite Madonna song, Papa Don't Preach.

Those fans who whinged to Madonna about the lack of old hits on the Drowned World tour may yet have cause to rue the attentive ear of their mistress, although most will be happy to sing along to hits such as Material Girl, no matter if they're reworked into jazz standards or death metal dirges.

The other news coming out of the show's early rehearsals is that the re-Invention tour will be less reliant on old-style Madonna theatrics. That makes good sense, especially considering the reviews currently being garnered by Spears, whose production-heavy Onyx Hotel tour is virtually a homage to the oversexed Madonna of the 1980s and 1990s.

The comparison between queen and would-be princess has never been so pertinent since that kiss and their subsequent duet on Spears's In The Zone album. And it's one which has the younger artist looking passe and out of her depth.

Spears's obvious hero worship on Onyx Hotel is apparent from her skimpy outfits to the faux coupling with Muscle Mary dancers to the faux masturbation scene. It's straight from the Madonna handbook, with Spears adding her only touch of originality by apparently lip-synching just about every song. Madonna, to her dubious credit, mimes only some of the time.

"It's official," wrote Pamela Stitt of The Seattle Times, reviewing the Onyx Hotel tour, "Britney is the new Madonna.

"Only Madonna did it better the first time around."

Joel Selvin, of the San Francisco Chronicle, wrote: "With the constant costume changes that emphasised skin over cloth, Spears came off as a cross between an aerobics instructor and a Victoria's Secret model.

"She probably didn't even sing live. The highly polished, processed and produced vocals were awfully smooth and clear to have been actually sung by someone who was huffing and puffing so hard just talking to the audience between numbers.

"Britney Spears is not about singing. She is a circus."

Said Gene Stout, of the Seattle Post-Intelligencer: "Only her production team knows how much she really sang."

While Spears plays at being Madonna redux and Madonna accepts her role as senior diva, the focus is increasingly turning to talented, original female artists who don't need to rely on tricks to command a concert stage.

Pink, who is touring Australia this month, earned solid reviews for her European tour which emphasised her songs and voice over her tomboy sexuality and the show business trappings required of major arena tours these days.

In America, a joint tour by Beyonce, Alicia Keys and Missy Elliott - who is touring Australia in May - has been a revelation, showing the strength and talent of three women who have made their way in a sexist, even misogynistic, hip-hop culture.

It's likely to be from this group of four that pop's next major female superstar will eventually emerge.

Kylie Minogue is faltering in her bid to conquer America and Christina Aguilera can't seem to shake her link to the lesser talented sexpot Spears, who looks destined to parlay her "singing" career into a glitzy Las Vegas act.

And for all her talk about reinvention, Madonna is an increasingly spent creative force. She could probably tour a greatest-hits show for years, a la the Rolling Stones, but like the Stones, fans will tolerate only so many new songs before the cry goes up for the safe and familiar.
