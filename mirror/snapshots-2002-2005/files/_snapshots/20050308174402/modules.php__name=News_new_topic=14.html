Music
Preview: ''Let The Music Flow''.

Rentboy Music Productions sent Drowned Madonna the brand new track fresh from their studios. "The track is titled “Let the music flow” and is homage to Madonna, having a good time and enjoying music", says Rentboy Music Productions.

The track was recorded and engineered by 21 year old Robert New, based upon samples from Madonna’s Vogue, he has updated the sound and wrote new melody and lyrics – also utilising famous stabs from Vogue.

"Due to be released in the summer of 2004, we wanted to give you a full free preview of our first release on our brand new music label run by young people in the UK," says Rentboy Music Productions. "The release will include remixes of “Let the music flow” and will be a non profit record. i.e. – this is our way of giving something back to people who love music. Rentboy Music Productions is producing young bands and artists who normally wouldn’t have the chance to or no how to get into a studio, record, write, promote and market themselves."

Robert New is a long time Madonna fan and a music maker. He’s always wanted to create something with a Madonna track - a remix or a cover - but instead was inspired to sample Madonna and Vogue and create a new dance track. "He was very much inspired by "Hollywood groove" & "Don’t wanna lose this groove" by Dannii Minouge -the track where Ms. Minouge sampled Madonna’s "Into the groove"" says the record label, which describes "Let the music flow" as a retro-dance track - a modernised piece of work encapsulating the sound of the London club scene mixed with retro synths and modern effects.

"We hope the track will be well received! A present to anyone who loves music - especially Madonna's," says Rentoby Music Productions. "There are also a few other projects in the pipeline which involve or are based upon a Madonna theme. We’re working on the remix’s of "Let the music flow" currently."

Benefit Concert Raises More Than 18 Million Dollars for Tsunami Victims

BURBANK, Calif. The stars who took part in a benefit concert for the South Asian tsunami victims managed to raise more than 18 (m) million dollars.

N-B-C Universal held the concert titled, "Tsunami Aid: A Concert of Hope," on January 15th. It aired on N-B-C, Bravo, U-S-A, the Sci-Fi channel, M-S-N-B-C, C-N-B-C, Trio, Telemundo and PAX.

N-B-C says the concert drew more than 19 and a-half (m) million viewers and raised about 18-point-three (m) million dollars.

Stars who performed included Madonna, Elton John, Sheryl Crow, Eric Clapton and Stevie Wonder. Celebrities who answered the phones to take in donations included George Clooney, Brad Pitt, Matt Damon and Ashley Judd.

The money is going to the Red Cross International Response Fund.

Madonna opens tsunami benefit concert
Pop diva sings ‘Imagine’ as celebs take donations from callers

LOS ANGELES - Madonna opened Saturday night’s benefit concert for tsunami victims with a haunting version of John Lennon’s "Imagine." The pop diva was joined by Maroon 5, Norah Jones, Mary J. Blige, Brian Wilson and Sarah McLachlan.

Elton John, performing live from London, sang one of his best known hits, "Don’t Let the Sun Go Down on Me".

Hip-hop star Nelly, Kenny Chesney and other musicians are also slated to perform during the commercial-free benefit, which is airing live on NBC and affiliated cable stations CNBC, MSNBC, USA, Bravo, Telemundo, Pax TV, Trio and Sci-Fi Channel. You can also watch it online at MSNBC.com.

MSNBC is a joint venture between NBC and Microsoft.

Actors Morgan Freeman, Clint Eastwood, Halle Berry, Robert De Niro, Meg Ryan, Kevin Spacey and Robert Downey Jr. gave testimonials, recounting the devastation and amazing survival stories of the victims of the Dec. 26 tsunami, which killed more than 150,000 in Asia and Africa.

Viewers can call 1-800-HELPNOW. All donations will go to the American Red Cross International Response Fund.

Fox News commentator Bill O’Reilly also appeared. He agreed to participate in the benefit after a much-publicized war of words with organizer George Clooney.

The concert was reminiscent of a similar benefit that ran on more than 30 television stations less than two weeks after the Sept. 11 terrorist attacks in 2001. That benefit raised nearly $130 million for that cause.

Sony’s Connect music store will offer downloads of live performances from the concert, with proceeds going to the American Red Cross. SBC Communications is underwriting the commercial-free program.

Mary J. Bligge sued for ''stealing'' Holiday.

Madonna’s status as an international icon of fashion, film and music is undisputed, and her signature hit, "Holiday," took the world by storm in 1983 and eventually became a classic.

Twenty years later, singer Mary J. Blige came out with "Not Today," with rapper Eve and produced by Dr. Dre. The song was featured in "Barbershop 2." But now Mary J, Eve, Dre and others connected to the song are being sued by the people who own the "Holiday" copyright, who think "Not Today" sounds a little too similar to Madonna’s song.

In court documents, the publishing company claims "Not Today" contains several unequivocal points of duplication, including sections of the rhythm and much of the harmonic content. But are they singing the right tune?

Judith Finnell, a New York musicologist and expert witness on copyright infringement who, in the past 20 years, has testified for the likes of Michael Jackson and Julio Iglesias, compared the two songs for "CJ." "It sounds similar enough to bear further consideration," she observed. "Both the pitches and the rhythms sound closely related. I don’t think they’re identical, because Madonna’s song is in what we call ‘major key’ and Mary J Blige’s song is in a minor key."

If this ever does go to a jury and jurors decide "Not Today" sounds significantly like "Holiday," the plaintiffs could win at least $15 million.

Source:  Celebrity Justice.
