[Rumours] Versace Photoshoot Rumour started weeks ago.

After the Brazilian website madonnaonline.com.br revealed the rumour about the new Versace ad campaign by Mario Testino, we contacted our insiders of Italian Fashion World. They told us that the news is indeed true and is passing from mouth to mouth for a long while in Italian fashion circles.

Here is an article from Fashionweekdaily published on October 8, 2004:

London Reunion: Versace, Madonna and Testino!
And a Parisian party for Paltrow

LONDON/PARIS: Friday, October 08, 2004

It looks like all those usual A-list celebs that grace the front row at Versace’s fashion shows opted to congregate for a dinner in London instead—you’ll notice by way of the recent Prada and Louis Vuitton store openings that dinner parties seem to have become all the rage, drawing in top celebs who later opt to skip the actual store party. Regardless, Donatella Versace, in town to introduce and launch her new fragrance, Crystal Noir (on counters in November), hosted a dinner for 30 people at Cipriani’s on Davies Street that brought out Madonna and her husband Guy Ritchie, Mario Testino, Elizabeth Hurley, Christian Slater (who arrived late) and Alexandra von Furstenberg and Tim Jeffries. In between plates full of food, Testino, ever the opportunistic lensman took advantage of the opportunity to table hop and snapped away. In between clicks, the topic of discussion amongst those in the candlelit room was the reunion of sorts between Versace, Madonna and Testino. You heard it here first: the trio discussed the possibility of planning something together in the near future, though it was unclear as to what that may be.

Commercials
Today is the day for Poles.
 Variety:

When 38 millions Poles switch on their televisions today, their eyes are bound to pop as they take in the Queen of Pop, Madonna herself, promoting local broadcaster Radio Zet.

The humorous ad, touted as the biggest-ever international star campaign for a Central European product, features Madonna tapping on the window of a limo driven by Zbigniew Zamachowski. The locally beloved Polish character actor, who starred in Krzysztof Kieslowski's "Three Colors/White," seems to have trouble believing his own eyes as Madonna purrs, "Are you my driver?"

The closest thing he can manage in reply, before taking her for a music video-style spin around Warsaw, is a heavily accented "Ma-done-nah?"

France's Lagardere Group, which owns the consortium behind Radio Zet with Advent International and Polish shareholders, would not disclose what the campaign cost or the deal cincher, but Lagardere president and CEO Jean-Christophe Lestra told Daily Variety that it constituted a regional first in terms of star draw.

The campaign was financed and originated by Radio Zet, said Lestra, who admitted some shock when the station's program director, Robert Kozyra, informed him of the campaign. "I was even myself very surprised," he said. "It's a great combination -- in Poland (Radio Zet) is a huge brand."

The No. 2 Polish commercial station, with 8 million listeners, launches the Madonna and Zamachowski ads today on all national television stations, in 22 glossy magazines and on Radio Zet itself, to run for at least six months, with expected penetration reaching 50 views for every Pole aged 15-49, Kozyra said.

The ad, crafted by the Bum agency of Warsaw, was produced by OTO Studio (Poland) and directed by Tomas Konecki. The campaign, involving more than 100 people in the U.S. and Europe, applies advanced CGI processing to superimpose Madonna on the streets of Warsaw, using footage from her videos including "Vogue," "Material Girl," "Frozen" and "Hollywood."

As for whether Madonna herself spent time on location in Warsaw, publicist Cesco van Gool of the Polish-Dutch Dragan Produkties said, "That's not the point."

Indeed, to Polish audiences familiar with Radio Zet's slogan "Only the Big Hits," the next six months may look like delivery time on the goods

Get behind the wheel of a new Corvette, and you’ll be a kid again.
From The Courier-Journal:

Your kids are grown, you’re getting membership offers from AARP and 30-year-olds keep calling you "sir."

But get behind the wheel of a new Corvette, and you’ll be a kid again.

At least that’s the message General Motors is trying to send with the first television ads for the 2005 model of the sports car built in Bowling Green, Ky.

Debuting yesterday, the Corvette commercial was the centerpiece of GM’s $60 million Olympics advertising push.

Directed by Guy Ritchie, Madonna’s husband and director of the movie "Snatch," the commercial follows the daydream of a young boy who imagines driving and flying the Corvette through a city. The commercial ends with the boy on a playground holding a skateboard while looking at a parked Corvette. A voiceover calls the vehicle "the official car of your dreams."

Art Spinella, president of CNW Marketing Research in Bandon, Ore., said the ad plays to Corvette’s typical buyer — a baby boomer trying to recapture his youth.

"Once upon a time, (the Corvette) tended to be owned by people in the middle 30s. Now, it’s the early 50s, which isn’t surprising given its price," Spinella said. "And if there’s one thing baby boomers don’t like, it’s the idea of getting old. ... When we do our studies on what cars make people feel young, Corvette usually tops that list."

Redesigned for the 2005 model year, the new Corvette went into production in July. Changes include a more powerful engine, a smaller frame and subtle changes to exterior styling.

GM executives said the Olympics ad blitz was a natural choice because of the large number of affluent viewers.

"The Olympics are the largest event of the summer, and they draw the nation’s attention for more than two weeks," said Kim Kosak, Chevrolet general director for advertising and sales promotion. "It’s a great place to celebrate the arrival of the new Corvette."
